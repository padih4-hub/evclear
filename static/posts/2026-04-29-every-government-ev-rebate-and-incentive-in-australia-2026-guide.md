**Body:**

Let's be real — government incentives are confusing. Every state does something different, the rules keep changing, and half the information online is already out of date. So we've done the research for you.

Here's exactly what's available right now in 2026, state by state. No fluff, no jargon — just the actual numbers and how to get them.

### The big one first — Federal FBT Exemption

Before we get into state-specific incentives, this is the one most Australians are missing out on — and it's worth thousands.

If you're an employee and your employer offers salary packaging or novated leasing, you can get a brand new EV with **zero fringe benefits tax**. That means you're buying the car with pre-tax dollars — saving you anywhere from $5,000 to $15,000 depending on your income and the car you choose.

It applies to battery electric vehicles priced under $91,387 GST inclusive for the 2025-2026 financial year. That covers most EVs on the market right now.

**Important:** As of April 2025, plug-in hybrids no longer qualify. Only full battery electric vehicles get the exemption.

If you're an employee and haven't looked into novated leasing yet — do it before you buy anything. A 20 minute conversation with a novated lease provider could save you thousands.

### State by State — What's Available Right Now

#### Queensland

No direct cash rebate currently. But Queensland offers reduced stamp duty on eligible EVs and registration concessions. Not as generous as it used to be but still worth factoring into your total cost.

#### ACT

The most generous state scheme in the country right now. The ACT offers up to **$15,000 in interest-free loans** for eligible EV buyers plus full stamp duty exemption. If you live in Canberra and you're buying an EV — this is a no-brainer. Check the ACT Government website for current eligibility requirements.

#### Northern Territory

A **$1,500 home charger rebate** is available until June 30, 2026. If you're in the NT and planning to install a home wallbox — claim this before it closes.

#### New South Wales

NSW has moved away from direct cash rebates and now focuses on stamp duty exemptions and reduced registration fees for eligible EVs. Check Service NSW for current thresholds as these update regularly.

#### Victoria

Similar to NSW — stamp duty concessions and registration discounts rather than direct cash. Victoria has been scaling back its direct incentives so always verify current offerings on the VicRoads website before buying.

#### South Australia

Stamp duty exemption on new EVs and plug-in hybrids. SA also offers some of the lowest EV registration fees in the country. Worth checking the RevenueSA website for the latest figures.

#### Western Australia

WA offers a stamp duty exemption on eligible EVs under a certain price threshold. Check the WA Department of Transport for current eligibility.

#### Tasmania

Registration and stamp duty concessions available. Smaller market but solid incentives relative to the state's size.

### The Luxury Car Tax threshold

If you're looking at a higher-end EV, fuel-efficient vehicles have a higher LCT threshold of **$91,387 for 2025-2026**. Anything under that avoids luxury car tax entirely — which is why you'll notice a lot of EVs are priced just below that number.

### Dealer and manufacturer incentives

On top of government incentives, many brands are currently offering:

* Cashback up to $3,000 on selected models
* Low-rate finance from as low as 0.88%
* Free home charger included with purchase

Always ask your dealer what manufacturer incentives are currently running before you sign anything. These change frequently and dealers don't always volunteer the information.

### The honest tip

**Incentives change.** What's available today might not be available in six months. The Electric Car Discount review results are expected mid-2027 which could change the federal FBT picture significantly.

If you're on the fence about buying — the incentive environment right now is genuinely good. Waiting too long might mean waiting out some of these benefits.

Always verify current incentives on your state government's official website before making any decisions. This guide is accurate as of April 2026 but we recommend double-checking before you buy.
