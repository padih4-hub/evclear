The 2026 Federal Budget was handed down last night and if you're considering an EV — or already own one — there's a lot to unpack. Here's a plain English breakdown of what was actually announced and what it means for Australian EV buyers.

- - -

### The Big One — FBT Changes Are Now Confirmed in Law

The phased wind-back of the EV FBT exemption is no longer just announced — it's now confirmed as part of the Budget and locked in legislatively. Here's the timeline:

**Until 31 March 2027** — full FBT exemption for all eligible EVs under the LCT threshold. Nothing changes yet.

**1 April 2027 to 31 March 2029** — full exemption only for EVs under $75,000. EVs between $75,000 and the LCT threshold drop to a 25% FBT discount only.

**From 1 April 2029** — all eligible EVs below the LCT threshold move to a permanent 25% FBT discount. Full exemption ends for everyone.

Existing salary packaging arrangements are to be grandfathered — so if you're already in a novated lease, the rules that applied when you signed should continue for the life of your arrangement. [EVSE Australia](https://evse.com.au/)

The Budget forecasts the changes will bring in approximately $1.9 billion over five years — which tells you just how popular and costly the original exemption became. [This is Electric](https://www.thisiselectric.com.au/learn/best-home-ev-chargers)

The message is clear: the window for maximum FBT benefit is open right now and has a firm expiry date. For anyone considering a novated lease on an EV under $75k, the full exemption runs until April 2029. For EVs between $75k and $91,387 — your window closes April 2027.

- - -

### Fuel Excise Discount Is Ending — Petrol Prices Going Back Up

Here's the news that affects petrol car owners most directly. The fuel excise discount introduced as relief for record fuel prices — which cut the excise from 52.6c/L to 20.6c/L — is ending on July 1, 2026 and was not extended in the Budget. [EVSE Australia](https://evse.com.au/knowledge-hub/best-ev-chargers-australia/)

That means from July 1, petrol prices will rise by roughly 32c per litre as the full excise rate returns.

For EV owners — this doesn't affect you directly. Your running costs stay the same. For anyone still on the fence about switching, this makes the running cost comparison even more compelling from July 1 onwards.

- - -

### No EV Road User Charge — For Now

Despite long-running debate about how Australia will replace declining fuel excise revenue as EV adoption grows, the government stopped short of introducing a national EV road user charge in this Budget. [EVSE Australia](https://evse.com.au/knowledge-hub/best-ev-chargers-australia/)

This is good news for EV owners and prospective buyers. Transport Minister Catherine King said the government doesn't want to disincentivise EV uptake "particularly right at the moment when we are seeing such a surge." [EVSE Australia](https://evse.com.au/knowledge-hub/best-ev-chargers-australia/)

The road user charge debate hasn't gone away — it'll come up again. But for now, EV drivers continue to pay no per-kilometre charge on top of their electricity costs.

- - -

### $40 Million for Charging Infrastructure

The Budget includes $40 million over four years for regional and kerbside EV charging projects — targeting the two biggest gaps in Australia's charging network: regional highways and apartment/kerbside charging for those without home charging access. [ProductReview.com.au](https://www.productreview.com.au/listings/evse-australia)

This won't transform the network overnight but it's a meaningful step toward making EV ownership practical for more Australians — particularly those in regional areas and apartment dwellers who currently find public charging the most frustrating part of EV ownership.

- - -

### EV Market Share Hit a Record 16.4% in April

Buried in the budget coverage but worth noting — EVs hit an unprecedented 16.4% market share in April 2026. That's nearly one in six new cars sold being electric. [EVSE Australia](https://evse.com.au/knowledge-hub/best-ev-chargers-australia/)

This is the context behind the government's decision to start winding back the FBT exemption — the policy did its job of accelerating adoption, and the government now sees EVs as mainstream enough to reduce the subsidy gradually rather than abruptly.

- - -

### What This All Means for You

**If you're considering a novated lease on an EV under $75k** — the full exemption is yours until April 2029. Three years of maximum benefit. The Budget has made this timeline certain.

**If you're considering a novated lease on an EV between $75k-$91k** — your window for full exemption closes April 2027. Less than two years.

**If you already have a novated lease** — you're grandfathered. The rules when you signed apply for the life of your arrangement.

**If you're a petrol car owner** — your fuel costs are going up from July 1 when the excise discount ends. The running cost gap between petrol and EV is about to get wider.

**If you're an EV owner** — no road user charge, charging infrastructure investment coming, and your running cost advantage just increased relative to petrol cars from July 1.

- - -

### The Bottom Line

The 2026 Budget didn't dramatically change the EV landscape — but it confirmed the direction of travel. The generous FBT exemption is being wound back on a clear timeline, petrol is getting more expensive, and the charging network is getting investment.

For anyone who has been considering an EV and putting it off — the Budget is another reason not to wait. The best version of the tax benefit is available right now, and from April 2027 it starts narrowing.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Did the 2026 Budget change the EV FBT exemption?**

Yes — it confirmed the phased wind-back announced earlier this year. Full exemption continues until April 2027 for all eligible EVs, then narrows based on price before ending entirely in April 2029.

**Will petrol prices go up after the Budget?**

Yes — the fuel excise discount ends July 1, 2026. Expect pump prices to rise by around 32c per litre from that date.

**Is there an EV road user charge in the 2026 Budget?**

No — the government chose not to introduce a national EV road user charge in this Budget, citing the need to maintain EV uptake momentum.

**Are existing novated leases affected by the FBT changes?**

No — existing arrangements are grandfathered under the rules that applied when the lease was signed.
