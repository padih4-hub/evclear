The novated lease FBT exemption gets most of the attention when people talk about EV incentives in Australia. And for good reason — it can save $13,000 to $25,000 over three years. But not everyone has access to salary packaging. So does buying an EV still make sense without it?

The honest answer is: yes, for most Australians — but the maths is different and the payback period is longer.

- - -

### What You Miss Without a Novated Lease

To be clear about what you're giving up. A novated lease for an eligible EV gives you:

* Lease repayments from pre-tax salary — reducing your taxable income
* Zero Fringe Benefits Tax on eligible EVs — worth thousands per year
* Running costs like rego, insurance, and charging bundled pre-tax

Without salary packaging access, none of that applies. You're buying or financing the car with after-tax money, the same as any other purchase.

That said, the running cost advantage of an EV has nothing to do with novated leases — and that's where the case for buying without one still holds up.

### The Payback Calculation Without a Lease

The honest question is how long it takes to recover the price premium of an EV over a comparable petrol car when you're paying with after-tax money.

Let's use a real example. BYD Atto 3 Premium at $44,990 versus a comparable petrol SUV at around $38,000. Price premium: roughly $7,000.

At $2,820 annual fuel saving (15,000km/year) plus $600 servicing saving — that's $3,420 per year better off running an EV.

Payback on the $7,000 premium: just over 2 years.

After that, every year you're $3,420 ahead. Over 5 years that's $17,100 in running cost savings on a car that cost $7,000 more upfront.

Without a novated lease the payback takes longer than with one — but it still makes financial sense for most buyers within a normal ownership period.

### Where It Gets Tricky Without a Lease

**Financing costs** — if you're taking out a car loan, interest adds to the total cost. Green car loans from some Australian lenders offer lower rates for EVs — worth shopping around before you commit to standard financing.

**No home charging** — the running cost saving assumes home charging at roughly 30c/kWh. If you're relying on public DC fast chargers at 50-65c/kWh, the saving shrinks significantly. Sort your charging situation before you buy.

**Lower price point EVs make more sense** — without the tax benefit amplifying the saving, buying a $32,000 MG4 makes more intuitive financial sense than stretching to a $55,000 BYD Seal. The price premium to recover is smaller and the payback is faster.

### State Incentives Still Apply

Even without a novated lease, you may still be eligible for state-based incentives:

* **ACT** — stamp duty exemption and 2 years free registration
* **NT** — free registration until June 2027, stamp duty concession on EVs under $50k
* **SA** — reduced stamp duty rate for EVs
* **WA** — reduced stamp duty tiered rate
* **QLD, NSW, VIC** — most cash rebates have closed but check current status at your state revenue office

These apply regardless of how you finance the car.

### The Best EVs to Buy Without a Novated Lease

Without the tax benefit, value for money at the purchase price matters more. Here's where to look:

**Under $35,000** — MG4 Urban, BYD Dolphin Essential. Lowest upfront cost means fastest payback.

**$35,000-$50,000** — BYD Atto 3, Geely EX5, Kia EV3. Strong range, good value, reasonable price premium over petrol equivalents.

**$50,000+** — the case gets harder without a lease as the price premium grows. Still makes sense long term but payback extends beyond 3 years for most buyers.

### Should You Buy Outright or Finance?

If you have the cash — buying outright removes interest costs and gives you full ownership from day one.

If you need to finance — look for a green car loan specifically. Several Australian lenders offer lower rates for EVs. Even saving 1-2% on interest over 5 years adds up meaningfully.

ACT offers stamp duty exemption and free registration. NT offers free registration and a stamp duty concession. SA and WA have reduced stamp duty rates. Most cash rebates in other states have closed.
