Mercedes-AMG just pulled the covers off its first purpose-built electric performance car and the numbers are genuinely staggering. The new GT 4-Door Coupe produces 860kW and 2,000Nm of torque in GT63 form, does 0-100km/h in 2.1 seconds, charges at up to 600kW, and has been confirmed for Australia.

The design, however, is already splitting opinion everywhere it's been posted.

This isn't an EV that's relevant to most Australian buyers — with pricing expected well north of $400,000 based on the outgoing V8 model — but it matters for a different reason. It signals where the performance car world is heading, and it does it in a way that's hard to ignore.

- - -

### The Specs — What Makes This Car Significant

The GT 4-Door Coupe is built on Mercedes-AMG's new AMG.EA 800-volt electric architecture. Two variants are confirmed — the GT55 and GT63.

Three axial-flux motors power the car — two at the rear and one at the front that acts as a boost unit and decouples when not needed to reduce drag. The axial-flux motors were developed by YASA, a British electric motor specialist acquired by Mercedes-Benz in 2021. Mercedes-AMG claims more than 35 world-first manufacturing techniques were required to produce them.

The 600kW peak DC charging rate is the headline technical achievement. At that rate, a 10 to 80 percent charge takes minutes rather than the 20-30 minutes most current EVs require. For context, the fastest public chargers in Australia currently run at around 350kW — the infrastructure will need to catch up before Australian owners can use the full charging speed.

### The Design — Where Opinion Divides

The outgoing GT 4-Door Coupe was a handsome car. Long bonnet, fastback roofline, understated aggression. Most people would call it a good-looking machine.

The new one is a different conversation.

Six circular taillights arranged in a turbine pattern. Corner-mounted headlights connected by a black strip under the bonnet. A long hood that AMG kept specifically to preserve the visual identity of the predecessor — unusual for an EV where there's no engine underneath requiring the space. Flush door handles, muscular rear haunches, and a roofline that slopes aggressively toward a wide tailgate.

It's a lot. Whether that's a lot of good or a lot of bad depends heavily on who you ask.

The design team has described it as "distinctly AMG" and a "masterpiece of engineering that heralds a new dimension in performance." The internet has been less diplomatic.

The honest take is that it photographs differently depending on the angle and lighting. Some shots look genuinely striking. Others look like something that needs a second look to process. It's not boring — and for a $400,000-plus car, boring might actually be worse than polarising.

### What This Means for the Australian Market

The GT 4-Door Coupe won't move the needle on Australian EV sales figures. At $400,000 plus it's a toy for a very small number of buyers.

But it matters for a different reason. Every time a major performance brand commits fully to electric, it accelerates the normalisation of EVs at every price point. Ferrari, Lamborghini, Porsche, and now AMG are all building their futures around electric drivetrains. That shift at the top of the market filters down to mainstream buyers faster than most people expect.

It also signals something important about electric performance — that the technology has now surpassed what combustion can do in straight-line performance terms. 860kW and 2.1 seconds from a four-door family car is a number that no V8 or V12 can match.

Mercedes-Benz Australia has confirmed the GT will be sold locally with orders opening in the coming days. Pricing, arrival timing, and equipment details have not yet been confirmed. Based on the outgoing GT63 S E Performance which was priced from $409,600 before on-road costs, expect the new fully electric version to land somewhere in that territory or higher given the technology step.

It will also attract Luxury Car Tax given the price point — though at $400,000-plus the LCT is the least of a buyer's concerns.

### Should This Change How You Think About EVs?

If you're in the market for a $50,000-$75,000 EV and watching AMG reveal a $400,000 electric supercar, it might feel irrelevant. But there's a connection worth understanding.

The technology that makes the AMG GT63 do 0-100 in 2.1 seconds — 800V architecture, axial-flux motors, ultra-fast charging — is the same technology that's filtering into mainstream EVs over the next 3-5 years. The Hyundai Ioniq 6 and Kia EV6 already use 800V architecture. The Zeekr 7X charges at 360kW. The pace of technology improvement in EVs is faster than any other segment of the car market.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**How much will the Mercedes-AMG GT 4-Door Coupe cost in Australia?**

Pricing has not been confirmed. Based on the outgoing GT63 S E Performance which was priced from $409,600 before on-road costs, expect the new fully electric version to be in a similar or higher range.

**When is the Mercedes-AMG GT 4-Door Coupe coming to Australia?**

Series production begins in mid-2026 at the Mercedes-Benz plant in Sindelfingen, Germany. Australian arrival timing has not been confirmed but orders are expected to open in the coming days.

**How fast does the Mercedes-AMG GT63 charge?**

The GT63 supports peak DC charging at up to 600kW, which is among the fastest charging rates of any production car. A 10 to 80 percent charge takes minutes at that rate, though Australian public charging infrastructure currently maxes out at around 350kW.

**Does the Mercedes-AMG GT 4-Door Coupe qualify for the FBT exemption?**

No. The FBT exemption applies to eligible EVs under the Luxury Car Tax threshold of $91,387. At $400,000-plus the GT 4-Door Coupe sits well above that threshold and does not qualify.

**What makes the axial-flux motors significant?**

Axial-flux motors are more compact and lighter than conventional radial-flux motors and can produce more power relative to their size. Mercedes-AMG developed them in partnership with YASA specifically for this application — it's the first series-production fully electric vehicle to use them.
