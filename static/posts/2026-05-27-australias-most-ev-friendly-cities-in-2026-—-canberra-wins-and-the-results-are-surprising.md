### Canberra — Why It Wins by Such a Large Margin

Canberra's score of 84.37 out of 100 puts it more than 21 points ahead of second-placed Perth. That's not a close race — it's a different league.

The numbers behind the score are striking. More than 3% of registered passenger and light vehicles in Canberra are now electric — streets ahead of every other capital. The city has more than 80 public charging connectors per 100,000 residents, also the highest in the country.

The ACT government's approach has been the main driver. Stamp duty exemptions, free registration, and a low-interest loan scheme created financial conditions that made EV adoption significantly more attractive than anywhere else in Australia. The result is a city where EVs are genuinely mainstream in a way they aren't yet in Sydney or Melbourne.

- - -

### Perth — The Surprise Second Place

Perth in second place is the result that surprises most people. Western Australia doesn't have the EV reputation of Sydney or Melbourne, but the combination of competitive public charging costs and strong infrastructure growth has pushed it above the larger eastern capitals.

The WA government's now-closed $3,500 ZEV rebate accelerated adoption, and the charging network has grown quickly to support it. Perth drivers benefit from lower public charging costs than Sydney on average, which improves the day-to-day ownership experience.

- - -

### Adelaide — The Renewable Paradox

Adelaide is the most interesting result in the study. South Australia generates more renewable energy than any other state — frequently running on 100% renewables for extended periods — yet EV adoption lags behind what you'd expect given those credentials.

The explanation is primarily economic. South Australia has relatively high electricity prices despite its renewable generation, and the state's EV incentives have been less aggressive than the ACT or WA. The infrastructure exists but the financial trigger hasn't been as compelling.

- - -

### Darwin — Australia's Least EV Friendly City

Darwin at the bottom of the rankings is largely a function of scale and geography. The city has just seven public EV chargers — the lowest of any Australian capital by a significant margin. For EV owners who need to rely on public charging for longer trips, Darwin's network simply isn't there yet.

The NT government has offered incentives including free registration and a stamp duty concession for EVs under $50,000, which helps with purchase economics. But the charging infrastructure gap means the day-to-day ownership experience in Darwin remains the most challenging of any Australian capital.

### What This Means for EV Buyers

Where you live genuinely affects how practical EV ownership is — and this study quantifies it for the first time in a rigorous way.

The good news is that the gap is closing. Every city on this list has more charging infrastructure than it did 12 months ago, and the pace of investment is accelerating as EV sales volumes grow. The bottom of the list today looks more like the middle of the list did two years ago.

For buyers in Canberra, Perth, Sydney, and Melbourne — EV ownership is now genuinely practical for most driving patterns. For buyers in Darwin and regional areas, home charging is essentially non-negotiable for the ownership experience to work.

The most important factor remains home charging access regardless of which city you live in. If you can charge at home overnight, the public charging network is a backup rather than a primary dependency — and that changes the practicality calculation significantly.

**Which Australian city is least EV friendly?**

Darwin ranks last with just seven public EV chargers in the entire city — the lowest of any Australian capital. Limited infrastructure makes day-to-day EV ownership more challenging than anywhere else.

### The Bottom Line

Australia's EV transition is happening at very different speeds in different cities. Canberra is operating in a different category to the rest of the country. Perth has punched above its weight. Darwin has a long way to go.

The common thread in every high-scoring city is government intervention — financial incentives and charging infrastructure investment that made EV ownership more practical and more affordable than the national average.

The gap between cities will narrow as EV sales volumes grow and infrastructure investment accelerates. But for buyers making decisions today, where you live — and specifically whether you can charge at home — remains the single biggest factor in how good your EV ownership experience will be.
