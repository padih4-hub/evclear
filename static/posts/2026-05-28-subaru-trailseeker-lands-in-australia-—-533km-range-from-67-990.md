Subaru has added a third electric SUV to its Australian lineup. The Trailseeker is now available to order from $67,990 before on-road costs — positioned as the electric counterpart to the beloved Outback, with more boot space, a longer wheelbase, and 533km of WLTP range from the base variant.

It's a significant launch for Subaru Australia, which is rapidly building out its EV range alongside the existing Solterra. For buyers who've been waiting for a Subaru EV that looks and feels like the brand they already trust — the Trailseeker is it.

### How It Compares to the Outback

The Trailseeker is 35mm shorter and 20mm narrower than the petrol Outback but sits on a 105mm longer wheelbase. Boot space is larger — 609 litres in the AWD and 595 litres in the AWD Touring, versus 530 litres in the Outback.

Ground clearance is 211mm — competitive with the Outback's 213mm and significantly more than most electric SUVs which sit much lower to the ground. The Trailseeker retains Subaru's Grip Control system and Multi-Terrain Monitor, giving it genuine light off-road capability that most EVs in this price range simply don't have.

For existing Subaru Outback owners who've been waiting for an electric version that keeps the practicality and light off-road capability they rely on — the Trailseeker is the closest thing available.

**Trailseeker AWD Touring adds:**

20-inch alloy wheels, semi-autonomous parking assist, V2L capability at 220V/1500W, panoramic glass roof, ventilated front seats, leather-accented upholstery, and a 10-speaker Harman Kardon sound system.

The V2L capability on the Touring is a standout feature at this price point — 1,500 watts is enough to run a laptop, small appliances, or power tools at a campsite. For Subaru's adventure-oriented audience, that's a genuinely useful addition.

### FBT and Novated Lease

Both Trailseeker variants sit under the $75,000 FBT threshold meaning full FBT exemption applies on a novated lease. For employees with salary packaging access, the novated lease saving on the Trailseeker AWD at $67,990 is significant — potentially $10,000 to $13,000 depending on income bracket.

The AWD Touring at $73,990 is borderline — drive-away pricing including on-road costs will push it close to or above $80,000 depending on state, which won't affect FBT eligibility but is worth noting for budget planning.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does the Subaru Trailseeker cost in Australia?**

The Trailseeker AWD starts from $67,990 before on-road costs and the AWD Touring is $73,990 before on-road costs.

**What is the Subaru Trailseeker's range?**

The AWD variant has a WLTP-rated range of 533km. The AWD Touring has a WLTP-rated range of 488km due to its heavier 20-inch wheels.

**Does the Subaru Trailseeker qualify for the FBT exemption?**

Yes — both variants sit under the $75,000 FBT threshold and qualify for full FBT exemption on a novated lease.

**How is the Subaru Trailseeker different from the Solterra?**

The Trailseeker has more Outback-like styling with a boxier, more upright body. It has more boot space, higher ground clearance, and faster DC charging at 150kW versus the Solterra's 100kW. It's priced slightly higher.

**When is the Subaru Trailseeker available in Australia?**

Pre-orders are open now with deliveries expected in the second quarter of 2026.
