Lexus just made one of the most dramatic pricing moves in the Australian EV market. The 2026 RZ electric SUV has been repriced with cuts of up to $42,059 — reductions of more than 30 per cent — and the entry level model now sits at $84,500 before on-road costs.

That's not just a significant discount. It's a threshold moment. The RZ500e Luxury at $84,500 now sits under the $91,387 Luxury Car Tax threshold, which means it qualifies for the FBT exemption on a novated lease for the first time.

### What's New in the 2026 RZ

The price cut isn't the only headline. The 2026 RZ arrives with meaningful technical improvements across the range.

The RZ500e gets a larger 74.7kWh battery — up 3kWh — which increases WLTP range by 16 per cent to 460km. AC charging has doubled to 22kW for faster top-ups away from DC stations. DC charging at up to 150kW takes 10 to 80 per cent in around 28 minutes.

The new RZ550e F Sport is the standout addition — a 300kW dual-motor variant with 437km of WLTP range and a 77kWh battery. Its headline feature is Australia's first steer-by-wire system paired with a yoke steering wheel. The yoke rotates from lock to lock in around 200 degrees, eliminating hand-over-hand steering. An Interactive Manual Drive mode allows paddle shifters to simulate eight gears with reproduced motor sounds and a virtual tachometer — Lexus's answer to buyers who want something that feels engaging without a combustion engine.

A limited edition RZ600e F Sport Performance is also confirmed for late 2026 with a 313kW powertrain, carbon fibre roof, and 21-inch Enkei alloys.

### The FBT Angle — Why This Matters for Salary Packaging Buyers

This is the part most coverage misses.

The previous RZ started at over $120,000 which put it well above the $91,387 LCT threshold. That meant no FBT exemption and no novated lease benefit — a massive disadvantage versus Chinese competitors that sit well under the threshold.

At $84,500 before on-road costs the RZ500e Luxury now sits under the threshold. For employees with salary packaging access, that changes the ownership maths significantly. The FBT exemption on a novated lease can save $10,000 to $16,000 depending on income bracket — turning a premium EV purchase into something that competes much more closely with mainstream alternatives on a total cost basis.

The RZ500e Sports Luxury at $91,000 is borderline — right at the LCT threshold. Drive-away pricing including on-road costs will likely push it over the $91,387 mark depending on state, which would affect FBT eligibility. Confirm with your novated lease provider before proceeding on that variant.

### Will It Actually Sell?

Lexus Australia is forecasting around 300 RZ sales for 2026. In a segment where the Zeekr 7X and Tesla Model Y variants shift thousands of units annually, that's a modest target.

The honest challenge for the RZ isn't price anymore — it's brand perception in the EV segment. Chinese brands have spent three years proving their reliability at lower price points. The Zeekr 7X at $57,900 to $72,900 offers more range, faster charging, and a more established EV-specific platform than the RZ at $84,500. Polestar 4 at a similar price point is a stronger EV-native competitor.

What the RZ offers that none of those alternatives can match is the Lexus dealership network, Lexus's legendary reliability reputation in Australia, and the Encore membership program covering roadside assist, service loan cars, and lifestyle experiences. For buyers who want Japanese reliability assurance in an EV and have been waiting for a price that makes sense — the 2026 RZ is the first version worth seriously considering.
