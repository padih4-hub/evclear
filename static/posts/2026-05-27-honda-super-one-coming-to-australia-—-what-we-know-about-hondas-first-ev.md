Honda has confirmed its first electric vehicle for Australia — and it's not an SUV. The Super-One is a compact city hatchback arriving in the second half of 2026, and it's genuinely unlike anything else in the Australian EV market right now.

Here's everything confirmed so far and what to expect when it lands.

- - -

### What Is the Honda Super-One?

The Super-One started life as the Japan-only N-ONE e: kei car, evolved into a slightly larger format for international markets including Australia, UK, and Europe. It's tiny — 3,600mm long and 1,600mm wide — making it smaller than every other EV currently on sale in Australia including the BYD Atto 1 and Hyundai Inster.

Honda describes it as a "Street Machine" for city living. The target buyer is someone who lives in an inner-city apartment, does short daily commutes, and wants something genuinely fun and characterful rather than just practical.

### The Boost Mode — Honda's Most Interesting Feature

The Super-One has a trick up its sleeve that no other EV in Australia offers. Boost Mode lifts power from 47kW to 70kW for sharper acceleration and simultaneously activates a simulated 7-speed gearbox with paddle shifters. The Bose sound system generates cabin sounds that respond to throttle input and braking — not fake engine noise exactly, but something Honda describes as a "driving sensation" that makes the car feel more engaging than a typical silent EV.

It's a deliberately fun feature aimed at buyers who love the feel of a manual gearbox and are nervous about losing that engagement in an EV. Whether it actually works in practice won't be clear until Australian journalists drive it — but the concept is interesting.

Australian pricing hasn't been confirmed. The best indication comes from Japanese pricing, which translates to approximately $30,000 AUD at current exchange rates. Honda Australia has positioned it below the Honda Civic which starts at $49,900 driveaway, and it's expected to be competitive with or undercut the Honda HR-V Hybrid from $39,900.

### Why the Range Is the Honest Concern

274km WLTP translates to roughly 200-220km of real-world range in Australian conditions. For a pure city car that charges at home overnight, that covers most daily use cases comfortably. But it leaves very little buffer for anything beyond the city — a Brisbane to Gold Coast run is around 75km each way, which is doable but tight on a single charge.

For buyers who exclusively drive within a major city and can charge at home, 274km is adequate. For anyone who occasionally needs more range, the BYD Dolphin at 340km and similar price point is a more practical choice.

The Super-One's value proposition is personality, brand familiarity, and the Boost Mode driving experience — not outright practicality. Honda knows this and isn't pretending otherwise.

- - -

### The Honda Brand Advantage

For buyers who are nervous about Chinese EV brands, the Honda badge is a genuine differentiator. Honda has decades of reliability data in the Australian market and an established dealer network. For someone who wants an affordable city EV and isn't ready to trust BYD or Leapmotor, the Super-One may be exactly what they're looking for regardless of the range trade-off.

This is Honda's first EV in Australia — which also means it's the first data point for how Honda handles EV ownership locally. Software updates, warranty claims, and service experience will all be watched closely by early buyers.

### FBT and Novated Lease

At an expected price well under $40,000, the Super-One would sit comfortably under the $75,000 FBT threshold and the $91,387 LCT threshold. If confirmed as a full BEV it would qualify for full FBT exemption on a novated lease until April 2029.
