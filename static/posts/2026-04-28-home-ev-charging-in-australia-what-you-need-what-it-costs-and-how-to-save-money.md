Let's be honest — one of the first things people worry about when buying an EV is charging. Where do I charge it? How long does it take? How much does it cost?

Good news — it's way simpler than most people think. And once you've got home charging sorted, you'll wonder why you ever stressed about it.

### First — the basics on charging speed

Before we get into options, let's decode two terms you'll see everywhere:

**kW (kilowatt)** — Think of this as the speed of charging. Higher kW means faster charging. A 7kW home charger is like a garden hose — steady and reliable. A 150kW public fast charger is like a fire hose — gets the job done in minutes.

**kWh (kilowatt-hour)** — This is your battery size. Think of it like a fuel tank. A 60kWh battery is bigger than a 40kWh battery and gives you more range — just like a bigger fuel tank gives you more kilometres per fill.

Simple as that. Now let's get into your options.

### Option 1 — Standard power point (slow charging)

This is the simplest option — plug your EV into a regular 3-pin 240V power outlet using the cable that comes with your car. Zero installation cost, zero setup.

The catch? It's slow. You'll get around 10 to 15km of range per hour. If you drive 50km a day you're looking at plugging in for 3 to 5 hours to recover that. For light daily use it works fine — but it's not ideal long term.

### Option 2 — Dedicated wall charger (the one we recommend)

A 7kW wallbox installed by a certified electrician gives you around 40 to 100km of range per hour. Most EVs can go from nearly empty to full overnight on a 7kW charger.

Think of it this way — a standard power point is filling your car with a cup. A wallbox is using a proper hose. You plug in when you get home, wake up full. Done.

**What does installation cost?**

Installing a wallbox in Australia typically costs between $1,000 and $1,500 all up — that includes the charger unit and installation by a certified electrician. Some EV brands throw in a free home charger with purchase so always ask before buying one separately.

Most Australian homes have single phase power which supports a 7kW charger. If you have three phase power you can go up to 22kW for even faster charging. Not sure which one you have? An electrician can check in 30 seconds.

### How much does it actually cost to charge?

Using average Australian electricity rates of around $0.30 per kWh — filling a typical 60kWh battery from empty costs around $18.

Compare that to $90 to $100 for a tank of petrol. That's not a typo. $18 versus $100.

**Two ways to make it even cheaper:**

**Off-peak charging** — Many energy retailers offer cheaper rates overnight. Charging between 11pm and 6am can cut your costs by 30 to 50%. Worth switching to an EV-friendly electricity plan before your car arrives.

**Solar charging** — If you have rooftop solar a smart charger can use your excess solar energy during the day. Many Australian EV owners charge almost entirely for free this way.

### What about apartments?

This is the trickiest situation for Aussie EV buyers right now. If you live in an apartment without dedicated parking, home charging is complicated. You'll need to contact your body corporate or strata manager about installing a charger — which can be a slow process in older buildings.

If you're apartment dwelling, factor in public charging as your primary option for now and check out our public charging guide for everything you need to know.

### The bottom line

Home charging is genuinely one of the best parts of owning an EV. You wake up every morning with a full battery. No servo runs, no waiting in line, no forgetting to fill up.

Get a wallbox installed before your car arrives, switch to an off-peak electricity plan, and you'll be paying around $18 to fill your car from empty. Every single time.
