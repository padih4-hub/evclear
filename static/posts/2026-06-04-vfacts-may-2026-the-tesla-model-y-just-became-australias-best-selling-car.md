Something historic happened in May 2026.

The Tesla Model Y became the best selling car in Australia. Not the best selling EV. The best selling car, full stop. Ahead of the Ford Ranger, the Toyota HiLux and the Toyota RAV4. An electric vehicle has never topped the monthly VFACTS charts before. May 2026 is the first time.

And if you look at the full picture behind that headline number, it gets even more interesting.

Total new vehicle deliveries in May came in at 106,887, down 2.3 percent on May 2025. But while the overall market was softer, EV and electrified vehicle sales went the opposite direction entirely.

Battery electric vehicles hit a record 20 percent market share for the month, up 111.6 percent compared to May 2025. Add in plug-in hybrids and conventional hybrids and electrified vehicles made up 46.4 percent of every new car sold in Australia in May. Nearly one in two new cars.

Petrol sales dropped 30.3 percent year on year. Diesel fell 26.2 percent.

**Why did this happen in May specifically?**

The fuel price crisis linked to the ongoing conflict in the Middle East is the main driver. When petrol prices spike hard and stay there, people start doing the maths on running costs. And right now the maths on EVs is compelling.

The EV Council's CEO put it simply: when fuel prices hurt, people look for alternatives. Electric vehicles offer no trips to the servo, no price spikes at the pump, and savings of around $3,000 a year for the average Australian driver.

That's exactly the kind of real-world calculation that tips people off the fence.

**It wasn't just Tesla**

Tesla had its best ever month in Australia with 6,433 total deliveries. The Model Y drove most of that. But BYD was the second biggest brand in the country with 8,211 sales across its full range, placing it ahead of every other manufacturer except Toyota.

The Geely EX5 came second among EVs with 1,814 units. The BYD Sealion 7 was third with 1,538 units, up 215 percent year on year. BYD placed five models in the top ten EVs for the month.

Chinese-manufactured vehicles, including Tesla and Polestar which are both built in China, accounted for roughly one in three new car deliveries in May. That's a number that would have seemed unthinkable two years ago.

The electric SUV segment led the charge. Compared to May 2025, electric SUV sales increased 167 percent. Plug-in hybrid SUV sales surged 377 percent.
