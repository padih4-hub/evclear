If you've been thinking about buying an EV but keep telling yourself you'll wait until the technology improves or prices drop, this article is for you.
It's a reasonable instinct. EVs are evolving fast and nobody wants to buy the wrong thing at the wrong time. But there's a real cost to waiting that most people don't factor in — and in 2026, the case for buying now is stronger than it's ever been.
Here's the honest breakdown.

The Market Right Now
Australia's EV market has matured significantly in the last two years. Prices have come down, the model range has expanded dramatically, and the charging network has improved across most of the country.

The days of EVs being a niche, expensive, limited-range product are over. You can now buy a genuinely capable, well-equipped EV from $32,990 drive away — and for most Australian driving needs, the technology is more than good enough.

The Argument for Buying Now
The FBT exemption window is open — but closing
This is the biggest reason to act sooner rather than later. The government has confirmed a phased wind-back of the EV FBT exemption starting April 2027. Right now you get full exemption on any eligible EV under the LCT threshold. From April 2027 that changes for EVs over $75k. From April 2029 it changes for everyone.
If you're an employee with salary packaging access, locking in a novated lease now preserves the current benefit for the full lease term. Waiting 12 months to buy could cost you $5,000-$10,000 in lost tax benefit depending on your income and the vehicle.
For the full breakdown of how the FBT changes affect your timing, read the complete novated lease guide on EVClear.
Prices have already dropped significantly
The "wait for prices to drop" argument made sense in 2022. In 2026 it's largely played out. BYD, MG, and other Chinese brands have already driven prices down to the point where EVs are genuinely competitive with petrol equivalents on total cost of ownership. Further significant drops are unlikely in the near term — and any savings from waiting will be partially offset by the narrowing FBT window.
The technology is good enough
Modern EVs from BYD, Tesla, Hyundai, and Kia are not first-generation technology. They have 400-600km of real-world range, fast charging, sophisticated software, and 8-year battery warranties. The next generation will be better — it always is — but the current generation is genuinely excellent for everyday Australian driving.
Waiting for the perfect EV is like waiting for the perfect smartphone. There will always be something better coming. The question is whether the current product solves your problem — and for most Australians, it does.
Running cost savings start immediately
Every month you delay is a month you're still paying petrol prices. At $2.00/litre and 10L/100km, 15,000km/year costs around $3,000 in fuel. An EV doing the same distance at home charging rates costs around $800. That's $2,200 per year in savings starting from day one.

The Argument for Waiting
To be fair, there are legitimate reasons to wait in some situations.
Your charging situation isn't sorted
If you live in an apartment without reliable charging access and public charging in your area is still patchy, waiting until your situation changes makes sense. Home charging is what makes EV ownership genuinely convenient — without it the experience is more frustrating.
You need a specific model that isn't here yet
If you've got your heart set on something that's not available in Australia yet — certain BYD models, upcoming Kia or Hyundai releases — waiting for the right car makes sense. Don't buy the wrong EV just to buy one.
Your financial situation isn't right
If you're not in a position to commit to a 3-5 year lease or finance a new car comfortably, waiting is the right call. Don't stretch yourself financially for a car.

What's Actually Coming That Might Be Worth Waiting For?
If you're genuinely on the fence, here's what's on the horizon in Australia:

More BYD models at lower price points
Improved public charging infrastructure particularly in regional areas
Potentially stronger resale values as EV adoption grows
Solid-state batteries — still 3-5 years away from mass production at minimum

None of these are compelling enough reasons to delay if you're otherwise ready to buy — particularly given the FBT timeline.

The Bottom Line on Timing
For most Australian employees with home charging access and salary packaging — now is genuinely one of the best times to buy an EV. The technology is mature, the prices are reasonable, the incentives are strong, and the window for maximum tax benefit is open right now.
The cost of waiting isn't zero. Every month you delay is fuel spending you didn't need to do, and every month from April 2027 onwards is a reduced tax benefit you can't get back.
