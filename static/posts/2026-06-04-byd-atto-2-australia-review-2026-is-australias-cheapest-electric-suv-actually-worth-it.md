Australia's cheapest electric SUV has arrived, and the number might surprise you.

The 2026 BYD Atto 2 starts at $31,990 before on-road costs. That's less than a Mazda CX-30, less than a Hyundai Kona, and less than a Toyota Corolla Cross hybrid. Except this one runs entirely on electricity.

BYD has been shaking up the Australian car market since 2022 and the Atto 2 is their most aggressive move yet. It slots below the popular Atto 3 and targets buyers who have been holding out for an EV that doesn't require a second mortgage.

But does cheap mean compromised? Let's get into it.

**What you get for the money**

There are two variants, both using the same drivetrain.

Both variants share the same 51kWh Blade battery, 130kW motor, and 345km WLTP range. The Premium is the one worth considering if it's your main car. The Dynamic makes more sense as a second car or city commuter.

**The specs that matter**

Motor: 130kW / 290Nm front-wheel drive Battery: 51kWh LFP Blade battery Range: 345km WLTP (expect 280 to 310km in real-world conditions) DC fast charging: 82kW, 10 to 80 percent in around 38 minutes AC home charging: 7kW, full charge in around 8 hours 0 to 100km/h: 7.9 seconds Charging connector: CCS2 (Australian standard) Warranty: 6 years vehicle, 8 years / 160,000km battery

That battery warranty is genuinely one of the best in the segment. Most competitors offer 8 years too but BYD's Blade battery technology has a strong track record for longevity.

**What it's like to actually live with**

The interior is a step up from what you'd expect at this price point. BYD brought in Wolfgang Egger from Porsche and Volkswagen to lead design, and it shows. The cabin feels modern and well-finished without trying too hard to be flashy.

The Premium's 12.8-inch rotating screen is a standout feature at this price. It tilts between landscape and portrait mode, which sounds gimmicky but is actually useful for navigation.

Real-world range sits between 280 and 310km depending on how you drive. On freeways you'll be at the lower end, in the city you'll get closer to the top. That's enough for most Australian daily commutes with room to spare, and with 82kW DC charging you can top up from 10 to 80 percent in about 38 minutes if you need it on a longer run.

But for pure value per dollar on an electric SUV in Australia right now, nothing touches the Atto 2.

**Is it eligible for a novated lease?**

Yes, and this is where it gets interesting.

At $31,990 before on-roads the Atto 2 is well under the $91,387 FBT exemption cap. That means if you have access to salary packaging through your employer, you can buy this car with pre-tax dollars and pay zero FBT on it.

For someone on a $90,000 salary, the after-tax saving on a novated lease over three years can be $10,000 or more depending on your usage and state. On a car that already starts at $31,990, that's genuinely significant.

**The verdict**

The BYD Atto 2 is a genuine achievement at this price point. It isn't perfect -- the Dynamic's 8-inch screen feels underwhelming and real-world range is closer to 280km than 345km in mixed driving. But for a primary city car or second family vehicle, it does everything it needs to do and costs less than most petrol small SUVs.

If budget is your main consideration, there is nothing cheaper in the electric SUV space right now. And with BYD's 8-year battery warranty backing it up, you're not taking a big risk to save money.

For most buyers the Premium at $35,990 is the sweet spot. The rotating screen, sunroof and heated seats make it feel like a proper car rather than a budget option, and you're still coming in well below anything else in the EV small SUV category.

**Who should buy it:** City commuters who want to go electric without spending $50,000+, second-car buyers, anyone on a salary packaging arrangement who wants maximum FBT saving per dollar spent.

**Who should look elsewhere:** Regular long-distance drivers, or anyone who needs more than 300km of reliable real-world range.

**Frequently asked questions**

**How much does the BYD Atto 2 cost in Australia?** The BYD Atto 2 starts at $31,990 before on-road costs for the Dynamic variant. The Premium is $35,990 before on-roads. Drive-away prices will vary by state but expect to add around $3,000 to $4,000 for on-road costs.

**What is the real-world range of the BYD Atto 2?** BYD claims 345km WLTP but real-world range in Australian conditions sits closer to 280 to 310km depending on speed, temperature and how you drive. Highway driving at higher speeds will put you at the lower end.

**Is the BYD Atto 2 eligible for the FBT exemption?** Yes. At $31,990 before on-roads the Atto 2 is well under the $91,387 FBT exemption threshold, making it eligible for a novated lease with zero FBT payable.

**How fast does the BYD Atto 2 charge?** The Atto 2 supports up to 82kW DC fast charging, giving you a 10 to 80 percent charge in around 38 minutes. On a 7kW home wallbox charger a full charge takes approximately 8 hours overnight.

**Is the BYD Atto 2 worth buying in Australia?** For buyers prioritising value and low running costs, yes. It's the cheapest electric SUV in Australia with a strong warranty and decent real-world capability. If range is your priority, models like the MG S5 or Leapmotor B10 offer more km per dollar spent on the battery.
