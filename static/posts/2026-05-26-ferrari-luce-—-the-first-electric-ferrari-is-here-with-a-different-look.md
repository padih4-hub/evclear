Ferrari unveiled the Luce in Rome today and it's the most significant electric car reveal of 2026. Not because of the specs — though those are extraordinary — but because of what it means when Ferrari, the most combustion-obsessed brand in the world, goes all-in on electric and says the car could only exist as an EV.

The Luce is Ferrari's first fully electric vehicle. It has four doors, five seats, four electric motors, 1,035 horsepower, and was designed in collaboration with Jony Ive — the man responsible for the look of every iPhone, iPad, and MacBook for two decades.

It's also €550,000. Roughly $AUD 950,000 at current exchange rates.

### Why This Car Matters Beyond the Price Tag

At close to a million Australian dollars, the Luce isn't relevant to almost anyone reading this. But it matters enormously for how people think about electric cars.

Ferrari's entire identity is built on combustion. V8s. V12s. Formula One. The roar of an engine that you feel in your chest. For Ferrari to not just build an EV but to say — as Ferrari CEO Benedetto Vigna did at the Rome reveal — that the Luce could only be the car it is because it's electric, is a cultural shift that goes far beyond the car itself.

When the most committed combustion brand on earth embraces electric and calls it a genuine performance advantage, it changes the conversation for everyone still on the fence.

- - -

### The Jony Ive Connection

The Luce's interior was designed by LoveFrom — the creative studio founded by Jony Ive and Marc Newson after Ive left Apple. The result is unlike any Ferrari interior ever made.

Physical switches and tactile controls sit alongside Samsung Display-developed glass panels with Corning Gorilla Glass. A 21-speaker, 3,000-watt sound system amplifies the actual mechanical frequencies from the electric motors — not fake engine noise, but real vibrations tuned and amplified into something that feels distinctly Ferrari without imitating combustion.

The cabin seats five — another first for Ferrari. The longitudinal engine and gearbox that previously prevented a fifth seat no longer exists, so Ive's team designed around the flat battery floor with rear coach doors that open as wide as the front items.

It's been described by journalists who saw it in Rome today as the most beautiful interior Ferrari has ever made. The exterior has divided opinion more — restrained by Ferrari standards, polarising by anyone else's.

- - -

### The Sound Question

One of the most watched aspects of any electric performance car is how it handles sound. Ferrari's answer with the Luce is interesting — they've built a physical accelerometer that amplifies actual mechanical motor frequencies inside the cabin. It's not artificial engine noise piped in through speakers. It's the real sounds of the electric drivetrain, amplified and tuned.

Whether that's enough to satisfy hardcore Ferrari fans who buy for the noise as much as the performance is a question that won't be answered until people actually drive one. First deliveries are expected in October 2026.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Ferrari Luce?**

The Ferrari Luce is Ferrari's first fully electric vehicle, revealed on 26 May 2026 in Rome. It produces 1,035 horsepower from four electric motors, seats five people, and costs approximately €550,000.

**Who designed the Ferrari Luce?**

The Luce's interior was designed by LoveFrom — the creative studio founded by Jony Ive, former Chief Design Officer at Apple, and Marc Newson.

**How fast is the Ferrari Luce?**

The Luce does 0-100km/h in 2.5 seconds with 1,035 horsepower from its quad-motor setup.

**What is the Ferrari Luce's range?**

Ferrari claims 530km of WLTP range from the 122 kWh battery.

**When will the Ferrari Luce be available in Australia?**

Ferrari has not confirmed Australian pricing or timing. First global deliveries are expected in October 2026. At approximately AUD $950,000 at current exchange rates, it will attract Luxury Car Tax and be limited to a very small number of buyers.
