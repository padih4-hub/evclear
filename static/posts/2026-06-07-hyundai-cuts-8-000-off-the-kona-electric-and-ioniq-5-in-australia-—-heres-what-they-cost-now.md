Hyundai just made two of Australia's most popular EVs significantly cheaper.

The Kona Electric and IONIQ 5 have both received permanent $8,000 price cuts across almost every variant in the range. This isn't an EOFY special or a run-out deal. These are the new permanent prices, effective immediately.

The Kona Electric now starts at $46,000 before on-road costs. The IONIQ 5 now starts at $68,200 before on-road costs. Both were $8,000 more expensive last week.

The IONIQ 5 N performance variant is not included in the price cuts and remains at its current price.

**What's the catch?**

There is one worth knowing about on the base models.

To hit the lower entry price, Hyundai has trimmed the standard equipment on the entry Kona Electric Standard Range. You no longer get a heat pump, an auto-dimming rear mirror, an interior V2L port, or a standard wall plug charging cable.

The heat pump is the most meaningful omission. It improves real-world range in cold weather by heating the cabin more efficiently than resistive heating. In Queensland this is less of an issue than in Victoria or Tasmania, but worth knowing if you are considering the base model.

The Elite and above still get full equipment. If you are buying a Kona Electric, the Extended Range at $50,000 is the sweet spot. You get the longer range battery and full equipment without paying for Premium trim.

**Why is Hyundai doing this?**

Chinese competition, plain and simple. The Kona Electric at $46,000 now competes more directly with the BYD Atto 3 at $39,990, the Leapmotor B10 at $37,888 and the Jaecoo J5 EV at $35,990. Hyundai has a stronger dealer network and more established service history in Australia, but the price gap with Chinese competitors has been too wide to ignore.

Hyundai's Australian boss also hinted there are more EV developments coming beyond today's announcement.

**Is it worth buying now?**

The Kona Electric is now one of the most compelling small electric SUVs from an established brand in Australia. If you want the peace of mind of a Hyundai dealer network, a proven platform and stronger resale history compared to newer Chinese brands, the $46,000 entry point is genuinely competitive.

For the IONIQ 5, the RWD at $68,200 now sits very close to the Tesla Model Y Long Range AWD at $68,900, and undercuts the Toyota bZ4X Touring and Subaru Trailseeker at $69,990. That is a meaningful shift in its competitive position.

Both models are well under the $91,387 FBT exemption threshold, which means they qualify for a novated lease with zero fringe benefits tax. Combined with the $8,000 price cut, the total saving for someone on a salary packaging arrangement is significant.

This is a meaningful price cut, not a gimmick. $8,000 off permanently on two of Australia's most popular EVs from one of its most established brands. The timing is driven by Chinese competition forcing Hyundai's hand, which is ultimately good news for Australian buyers.

The Kona Electric Extended Range at $50,000 is the pick of the range for most buyers. Full equipment, longer range, and $8,000 cheaper than it was last week.
