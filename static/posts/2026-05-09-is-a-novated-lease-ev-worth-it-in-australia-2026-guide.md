If you've been researching EVs in Australia and someone brought up a novated lease, you've probably asked yourself: is a novated lease EV actually worth it, or does it just sound better than it is?

Short answer: for most Australian employees with access to salary packaging, yes — it's genuinely one of the smartest financial moves you can make when buying a car right now. But it's not for everyone, and the details matter.

Here's the honest breakdown.

- - -

**What Makes a Novated Lease EV Worth It**

A novated lease isn't just a financing option. It's a tax structure. And when it works, it works hard.

Here's what's happening:

Your lease payments come out of your pre-tax salary. That lowers your taxable income, which means less income tax. On top of that, eligible EVs are currently exempt from Fringe Benefits Tax — a tax your employer would normally pay on non-cash benefits. That exemption alone is worth thousands per year.

For a typical Australian earning $100,000 and leasing a $62,000 BYD Seal over 3 years, the combined saving lands somewhere between $13,000 and $18,000. That's not a marketing number — that's what the tax maths actually produces.

And it doesn't stop at the lease payment. Running costs — rego, insurance, servicing, tyres, and electricity for charging — can all come out of pre-tax salary too. You're effectively paying for fuel and insurance with money you haven't paid tax on yet.

**When Is a Novated Lease EV NOT Worth It?**

It's not the right move for everyone. Here's when it probably doesn't make sense:

**Your employer doesn't offer salary packaging.** This is the dealbreaker. No salary packaging, no novated lease. Ask HR before you go any further — it's a simple yes or no.

**You're a contractor or sole trader.** Novated leases are an employee benefit. If you're self-employed, this doesn't apply to you.

**You're planning a job change soon.** If you leave mid-lease and your new employer doesn't offer salary packaging, the lease reverts to you personally. You keep the car but lose the pre-tax benefit. If your employment situation is uncertain, a shorter lease term or buying outright might be smarter.

**You have HECS debt or receive government benefits.** Even though the EV is FBT-exempt, it still appears on your income statement as a reportable fringe benefit. That can affect your HECS repayment threshold, Medicare Levy Surcharge, and Centrelink eligibility. Talk to an accountant first if any of those apply.

**How Much Can You Actually Save on a Novated Lease EV?**

Let's use a real example.

You earn $100,000. You're looking at a $62,000 BYD Seal Premium on a 3-year novated lease.

Without the FBT exemption, your employer would owe roughly $5,828 per year in FBT — costs that flow back to you through higher lease pricing. Over 3 years that's $17,484.

With the exemption — zero.

On top of that, your lease repayments come from pre-tax salary. At a 32% effective tax rate on $14,000 in annual repayments, you're saving roughly $4,480 per year in income tax.

**Total saving over 3 years: approximately $13,000 to $18,000.**

Your exact figure depends on your income, the vehicle price, lease term, and provider fees. Run your own numbers at [evclear.com.au/calculator](https://evclear.com.au/calculator) before you talk to any provider.

**Which EVs Qualify for the FBT Exemption?**

Not every EV qualifies. The key rules:

* Must be a full Battery Electric Vehicle — PHEVs lost the exemption for new leases from April 2025
* Must be under $91,387 at first retail sale — this is the LCT threshold for fuel-efficient vehicles
* Accessories added before delivery count toward the LCT value — so watch the total drive-away price carefully

Popular EVs that typically qualify include the BYD Seal, BYD Atto 3, Tesla Model 3, Tesla Model Y, Hyundai Ioniq 5, Ioniq 6, Kia EV6, MG4, Polestar 2, and Volvo EX30.

**What's Happening With the FBT Exemption?**

The government has confirmed a phased wind-back. Here's what's locked in:

**Until 31 March 2027** — full exemption still applies for all eligible EVs under the LCT threshold. Nothing changes yet.

**1 April 2027 to 31 March 2029** — EVs under $75k keep the full exemption. EVs between $75k and the LCT threshold drop to a 25% FBT discount only.

**From 1 April 2029** — full exemption ends for everyone. All eligible EVs below the LCT threshold move to a 25% discount permanently.

Existing leases are expected to be grandfathered, so locking in sooner preserves better terms for the full lease period.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

**Is a novated lease EV worth it in Australia in 2026?** For most permanent employees earning above $70,000 with access to salary packaging — yes. The combination of zero FBT and pre-tax payments is hard to beat.

**What happens if I change jobs mid-lease?** Your new employer may take over the lease — most large employers will. If they don't offer salary packaging, the lease reverts to you personally and you lose the pre-tax benefit but keep the car.

**Can I include charging costs in a novated lease?** Yes — ongoing electricity costs from a metered home charger can be bundled. The charger hardware itself cannot.

**Will the FBT exemption end?** Yes — the government has confirmed a phased wind-back. Full exemption runs until March 2027 for all EVs, then splits based on price, before a full 25% discount structure from April 2029. Existing leases are expected to be grandfathered.
