If you've ever looked at an EV's specs and seen "500km range" then wondered why owners report getting 380km in real life — this guide is for you.

Range is probably the most misunderstood number in the EV world. And if you buy a car based on the wrong number, you're going to have a bad time.

Here's what actually matters.

#### The WLTP number is not your number

Every EV sold in Australia is tested under something called the WLTP standard — Worldwide Harmonised Light Vehicle Test Procedure. It's a lab test designed to give a consistent, comparable figure across all vehicles.

The problem? It's tested in ideal conditions. Moderate temperature, moderate speed, no air conditioning, no passengers, flat road.

That is not Australia.

In real Australian conditions — Queensland summer heat, highway speeds, aircon running flat out — you'll typically see 10 to 20% less range than the official WLTP figure. Sometimes more.

So a car rated at 500km WLTP might deliver 400 to 430km in real Australian driving. That's still excellent — but it's the number you need to plan around, not 500km.

#### What actually affects your range

**Speed** is the biggest factor. EV efficiency drops significantly above 100km/h. At 110km/h you might use 30 to 40% more energy per kilometre than at 60km/h. If you do a lot of highway driving, plan for the lower end of your range estimate.

**Temperature** matters more than most people realise. Both hot and cold weather reduce battery efficiency. In a Queensland summer, your battery management system works harder to keep the battery cool — which uses energy. In cold climates, heating the cabin draws from the same battery pack.

**Air conditioning** is the hidden range killer. Running aircon at full blast in summer can reduce your range by 10 to 15%. Not a dealbreaker — but worth knowing.

**Load and passengers** make a difference too. A fully loaded car with four adults and luggage will use more energy than the same car with one person and an empty boot.

**Driving style** is entirely in your control. Smooth acceleration, gentle braking, and using regenerative braking well can significantly extend your real-world range beyond what aggressive drivers achieve in the same car.

#### The ranges that actually matter in Australia

Here's a practical way to think about range for Australian drivers:

**Under 300km real-world range** — Fine for city commuters who charge at home every night. Not ideal if you regularly drive regional routes or need flexibility.

**300 to 450km real-world range** — The sweet spot for most Australians. Covers daily commuting easily with plenty of buffer, and handles most weekend trips without needing to charge en route.

**450km+ real-world range** — Gives you genuine road trip capability on major Australian corridors. Brisbane to Sydney in two charging stops. Melbourne to Adelaide comfortably.

#### How to find the real range for your car

Before buying any EV, do this:

Search for "\[car model] real world range Australia" on YouTube and Reddit. Australian owners regularly share their actual consumption figures in local conditions. This is far more useful than any spec sheet.

The EV Database (ev-database.org) also shows adjusted real-world range estimates that account for real conditions — usually closer to what you'll actually experience than the official WLTP number.

#### The 80% rule

Most experienced EV drivers follow a simple rule — they charge to 80% for daily use and only charge to 100% when they need maximum range for a longer trip. This is because the last 20% of charging slows down significantly and daily charging to 100% can slightly accelerate battery degradation over time.

The practical result? If your car has 450km WLTP range and you follow the 80% rule, your usable daily range is closer to 300 to 320km. For most Australian commuters that's still far more than they'll ever need in a day.

#### The honest bottom line

Range anxiety is real — but for most Australian city and suburban drivers, it's also unnecessary. The average Australian drives under 50km per day. Even the most affordable EVs on the market today handle that with enormous headroom.

The drivers who genuinely need to think carefully about range are those who regularly drive long distances through regional or remote Australia. For everyone else — pick a car with at least 300km real-world range, charge at home overnight, and you'll never think about range again.
