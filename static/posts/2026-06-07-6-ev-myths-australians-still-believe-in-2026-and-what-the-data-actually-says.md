A Victorian Parliament inquiry published this week found something pretty telling.

Misinformation about electric vehicles is one of the biggest reasons Australians are not switching. Not price. Not charging infrastructure. Not range. Misinformation.

The Legislative Council's Economy and Infrastructure Committee identified six persistent myths affecting buyer confidence in Australia: range anxiety, charging availability, battery lifespan, resale value, fire safety, and environmental impact.

**Myth 1: EVs can't go far enough on a charge**

This is the big one. Range anxiety has been the number one concern for Australian EV buyers for years, and the inquiry found it is almost always overstated.

Modern EVs sold in Australia in 2026 typically offer between 400 and 500 kilometres of WLTP range. Some models exceed 600 kilometres. The early EVs from the 2010s that averaged around 100 kilometres per charge are a completely different product to what you can buy today.

For context, the average Australian driver covers around 38 kilometres per day. Even the cheapest EV on the market right now, the BYD Atto 2, offers 345 kilometres WLTP. That is nine days of average Australian driving on a single charge.

The real-world number is lower than WLTP in most cases, typically 80 to 85 percent of the rated figure. But even accounting for that, the gap between what people fear and what modern EVs actually deliver is enormous.

**Myth 2: There are not enough chargers in Australia**

Charging infrastructure is improving fast, and for most EV owners the question is almost irrelevant because they charge at home overnight.

If you have off-street parking, you plug in when you get home and wake up with a full battery every morning. You never visit a petrol station again. The public charging network matters primarily for long-distance travel, and even there the situation has improved significantly.

Fast charging infrastructure now covers every major Australian highway corridor. The inquiry found the infrastructure already exists to make long-distance driving viable. The perception of inadequacy is running well behind the reality.

**Myth 3: EV batteries die after a few years**

This one causes a lot of unnecessary hesitation, and the data does not support it.

Most EV manufacturers now offer 8-year battery warranties covering a minimum state of health, typically 70 percent capacity retention. Real-world data from high-mileage Tesla vehicles shows battery degradation of around 10 to 15 percent after 300,000 kilometres. That is not dying. That is barely noticeable for most Australian drivers.

LFP chemistry batteries, which are now standard in BYD vehicles and some Tesla models, are particularly durable and can handle daily charging to 100 percent without meaningful degradation. The battery technology in 2026 is genuinely different to what it was five years ago.

**Myth 4: EVs have terrible resale value**

This one is partially true and worth being honest about.

The Victorian Parliament inquiry acknowledged that resale values for EVs have been lower than petrol equivalents in recent years. Two-year-old EVs have been retaining around 65 percent of their new value compared to about 85 percent for passenger vehicles overall.

However, the inquiry also heard from the Australian Automotive Dealer Association that resale values are now improving as the market matures. As more Australians buy EVs, the pool of used EV buyers grows with it. The resale gap is narrowing.

**Myth 5: EVs are a fire risk**

EV fires get covered heavily in the media because they are unusual and dramatic. The data tells a different story.

Studies from the United States and Europe consistently show EVs catch fire at a lower rate per vehicle than petrol cars. The National Transportation Safety Board in the US found internal combustion engine vehicles are roughly 60 times more likely to catch fire than EVs per mile driven.

EV fires, when they do occur, can be harder to extinguish due to thermal runaway in battery cells. But the probability of one starting in the first place is significantly lower than with a petrol vehicle.

**Myth 6: EVs are not actually better for the environment**

The "dirty electricity" argument comes up constantly and it does not hold up to scrutiny in the Australian context.

Even on Australia's current electricity grid, which still relies heavily on coal in some states, an EV produces significantly lower lifecycle emissions than a petrol car. As the grid gets cleaner, which it is doing rapidly, that advantage compounds.

In Queensland specifically, rooftop solar penetration is among the highest in the world. If you charge your EV during the day from solar, your running emissions are effectively zero. The lifecycle emissions argument against EVs is weakest in exactly the states where solar adoption is strongest.

**The bottom line**

The Victorian Parliament inquiry got it right. Misinformation is holding Australians back from a switch that makes financial sense for most households right now.

**Frequently asked questions**

**Do EV batteries really last as long as manufacturers claim?** Yes, in most cases. Real-world data from high-mileage EV fleets shows battery degradation of around 10 to 15 percent after 300,000 kilometres. Most manufacturers back this up with an 8-year battery warranty covering a minimum state of health.

**Are EVs really a fire risk in Australia?** EVs catch fire at a lower rate than petrol vehicles per kilometre driven. EV fires receive more media coverage because they are unusual, not because they are more common.

**Do EVs hold their value in Australia?** Resale values for EVs have been lower than petrol equivalents in recent years, but the gap is improving as the used EV market matures. Buying through a novated lease changes the total cost of ownership calculation significantly.

**Is the Australian electricity grid clean enough for EVs to make environmental sense?** Yes. Even on the current Australian grid, EVs produce lower lifecycle emissions than petrol cars. In states with high solar penetration like Queensland, charging from rooftop solar makes the environmental case even stronger.

**How far can modern EVs travel on a single charge in Australia?** Most EVs sold in Australia in 2026 offer between 400 and 500 kilometres of WLTP range. Real-world range is typically 80 to 85 percent of that figure depending on speed, temperature and driving style.
