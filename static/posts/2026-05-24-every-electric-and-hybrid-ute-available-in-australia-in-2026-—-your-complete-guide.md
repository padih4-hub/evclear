### The GWM Cannon Alpha PHEV — Australia's Cheapest Electrified Ute

At $54,490 drive-away the GWM Cannon Alpha PHEV is currently the most affordable electrified ute in Australia — and GWM is running an EOFY discount until May 31 that brings the Lux variant down to $54,490 and the top-spec Ultra to $59,490.

The plug-in hybrid system gives around 100km of pure EV range for daily commuting before the petrol engine kicks in for longer trips. For tradies and farmers who do a predictable daily route and can charge at home overnight, that 100km EV range covers most day-to-day driving without touching the fuel tank.

The honest limitation is towing capacity in pure EV mode. Like most PHEVs, towing heavy loads drops the EV range significantly and the petrol engine carries most of the load. If towing is a daily requirement the PHEV benefit shrinks.

### Ford Ranger PHEV — The Mainstream Pick

Ford finally brought the Ranger PHEV to Australia and it's the most recognisable nameplate in the electrified ute segment. Starting from $67,490 drive-away it's more expensive than the GWM but carries the benefit of Ford's established dealer network and the Ranger's proven reliability reputation.

The EV range of around 45km is the weak point — significantly less than the GWM or BYD alternatives. For buyers who want an electrified Ranger more for the fuel saving on shorter trips than genuine EV driving, it still makes sense. For buyers wanting meaningful electric range, the GWM and BYD options are more compelling.

### BYD Shark 6 — The Premium PHEV Option

The BYD Shark 6 is the most technologically advanced PHEV ute in Australia right now. Starting from $69,900 it delivers around 100km of pure EV range — matching the GWM at a higher price point — but with BYD's more polished interior, stronger performance figures, and the brand's proven reliability in the Australian market.

For tradies who want a capable work ute with genuine EV range and aren't put off by the BYD badge, the Shark 6 is worth serious consideration. BYD has delivered 100,000 vehicles in Australia already — the brand is no longer a question mark.

Worth noting — the Shark 6 is a PHEV, not a full BEV, so it doesn't qualify for the FBT exemption that applies to eligible full electric vehicles. For employees on salary packaging looking to maximise the tax benefit, the full BEV options below are worth comparing.

### KGM Musso EV — Australia's First Full Electric Ute

The KGM Musso EV holds the distinction of being Australia's first proper full battery electric ute. Starting from around $60,000 it offers approximately 420km of WLTP range — genuinely useful for most Australian driving patterns.

The limitation is that at $60,000 it sits above the $91,387 LCT threshold... actually, wait — it's under the LCT threshold. But the FBT exemption applies to vehicles under $91,387 at first retail sale for eligible EVs. Confirm eligibility with your novated lease provider as the KGM is a newer entrant and FBT eligibility depends on specific vehicle classification.

The KGM brand is less established in Australia than Toyota, Ford, or BYD which raises reasonable questions about parts availability and long-term support. Worth factoring into the decision alongside the specs.

### Toyota HiLux BEV — The Name Everyone Recognises

Toyota's HiLux BEV is the most anticipated electrified ute in Australia simply because of what the HiLux nameplate means to Australian buyers. Starting from $74,990 for the SR variant and $84,990 for the SR5, it's the premium end of the electrified ute market.

The 315km WLTP range is the weakest of the full BEV options and the price is the highest. But Toyota's dealer network, parts availability, and brand trust are unmatched in the ute segment. For buyers who want zero range anxiety about getting their ute serviced in a remote area, Toyota's network is a genuine differentiator.

The HiLux BEV sits above the $75,000 FBT threshold but under the $91,387 LCT threshold, meaning full FBT exemption applies only until April 2027 for this model. After that it drops to a 25% discount. ### Should You Buy an Electrified Ute Now or Wait?

For most buyers the honest answer is to wait — unless one of the current options genuinely suits your needs.

The GWM Cannon Alpha PHEV and BYD Shark 6 are the best current options for buyers who want meaningful EV range at a reasonable price. The Ford Ranger PHEV suits buyers who prioritise the Ranger nameplate over maximum EV range. The Toyota HiLux BEV suits buyers who need Toyota's dealer network and are prepared to pay a premium.

If your primary concern is the novated lease FBT benefit, the full BEV options are where the tax saving is strongest — but check eligibility and LCT threshold status for each specific model with your provider.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**What electric utes are available in Australia in 2026?**

The main options are the GWM Cannon Alpha PHEV from $54,490, Ford Ranger PHEV from $67,490, BYD Shark 6 from $69,900, KGM Musso EV from around $60,000, and Toyota HiLux BEV from $74,990.

**Do electric utes qualify for the FBT exemption in Australia?**

Full BEV utes under the LCT threshold of $91,387 are eligible. PHEV utes like the GWM, Ford Ranger, and BYD Shark 6 do not qualify for the full FBT exemption. Confirm eligibility with your novated lease provider.

**What is the cheapest electric ute in Australia?**

The GWM Cannon Alpha PHEV is currently the most affordable at $54,490 drive-away with an EOFY discount running until May 31, 2026.

**Is the Toyota HiLux available as an electric ute?**

Yes — the Toyota HiLux BEV is available from $74,990 for the SR variant with approximately 315km of WLTP range.

**What electric utes are coming to Australia?**

The Deepal E07, JAC Hunter PHEV, Chery KP31 PHEV, LDV eTerron 9, and Isuzu D-Max EV are all confirmed or expected for Australia in late 2026 or 2027.
