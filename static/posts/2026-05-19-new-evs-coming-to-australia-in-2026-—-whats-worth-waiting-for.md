### What's Actually Coming to Australia in 2026

#### Mazda 6e — From $49,990

The Mazda 6e is one of the most anticipated arrivals of 2026. Starting from $49,990 plus on-road costs with a 78kWh battery and around 560km of WLTP range, it sits in a sweet spot that Mazda loyalists have been waiting for.

It's under both the $75,000 FBT threshold and the $91,387 LCT threshold — meaning full FBT exemption on a novated lease. If you're a Mazda fan who's been holding off, this one is genuinely worth waiting for. Expected in Australian showrooms mid-2026.

#### Honda e:N2 / Super-One — Under $40,000

Honda's first real EV push in Australia is expected under $40,000 with around 410km of range. If the pricing holds, it would be one of the most affordable EVs from a mainstream Japanese brand in Australia.

Worth noting: Honda's EV track record globally has been patchy. Wait for real-world Australian reviews before committing.

#### Mercedes-Benz CLA Electric

Mercedes has confirmed a five-variant lineup of the new CLA compact sedan coming to Australia including two fully electric models. Premium, well-equipped, and likely to sit above $75,000 — so the FBT calculation changes from April 2027. If you're considering a premium EV, this is worth watching but probably not worth waiting for if you need a car now.

#### Kia EV4

Kia's EV4 has already launched in some markets and Australian pricing is expected to be competitive — likely in the $50,000-$65,000 range. Same 800V charging platform as the Ioniq 6 and EV6 which means extremely fast charging speeds. Strong candidate if you want Kia's quality and charging speed at a mid-range price.

#### BYD Sea Otter (Racco)

BYD's smallest and most affordable EV yet — details are still emerging but it's expected to undercut the current Atto 1 on price. If you're looking at the sub-$30,000 segment, this could be worth waiting for. Timing for Australia is still unclear.

#### Xpeng People Mover

Xpeng's luxury people mover has been approved for sale in Australia. Niche but worth knowing if you need a large family EV beyond the current LDV Mifa 9.

### Is Waiting Actually Worth It?

Honest answer — usually no, for most buyers.

Here's why. Every month you wait is:

* Another month paying petrol prices — at current prices that's $200-400 in fuel depending on how much you drive
* Another month without the FBT tax benefit if you're on a novated lease
* One month closer to April 2027 when the FBT exemption starts narrowing

The Mazda 6e is the one exception where waiting makes genuine sense — if you're a Mazda buyer, it's arriving soon enough and priced well enough to justify holding out a few months.

For everyone else, the cars available today — BYD Seal, Tesla Model 3, Hyundai Ioniq 6, Kia EV5 — are genuinely excellent and the financial case for buying now rather than waiting is strong.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**What new EVs are coming to Australia in 2026?**

The most significant arrivals include the Mazda 6e from $49,990, Honda's first affordable EV under $40,000, the Mercedes CLA Electric, and the Kia EV4. BYD also has several new models in the pipeline.

**Is it worth waiting for a new EV model in Australia?**

For most buyers — no. The financial cost of waiting (fuel, lost FBT benefit, narrowing exemption window) usually outweighs any advantage from a newer model. The Mazda 6e is the one 2026 arrival genuinely worth waiting for.

**Will new EV models be cheaper than current ones?**

Slightly — competition is driving prices down gradually. But the savings from switching now typically outweigh any future price reduction, especially with the FBT exemption window closing.

**When is the Mazda 6e coming to Australia?**

Mid-2026 is the expected timeline for Australian showrooms, priced from $49,990 plus on-road costs.
