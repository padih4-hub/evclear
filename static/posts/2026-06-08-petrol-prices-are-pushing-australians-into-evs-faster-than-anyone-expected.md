When petrol prices jump, Australians pay attention.

It happened in early 2026 when the US-Israel conflict with Iran disrupted global oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz. Petrol surged past $2.50 per litre in major hubs. Diesel spiked past $3.00 per litre. Hundreds of service stations ran dry as panic buying doubled demand overnight in some areas. At the peak, over 400 stations nationally reported shortages.

And while Australians were lining up at servos, something else was happening at car dealerships.

EV sales went through the roof.

**The numbers tell the story**

New EV sales in March 2026 hit 15,839 units. That is a 43.3 percent jump on February and an 88.9 percent increase compared to March 2025, according to <a href="https://zecar.com/reviews/used-ev-sales-doubled-march-2026-australia" style="color:`#a8ff78`;border-bottom:1px solid rgba(168,255,120,0.3);">data from the FCAI and Electric Vehicle Council</a>. The overall new car market was actually down 2.6 percent that same month, which makes the EV result even more remarkable.

The used EV market was even wilder. That momentum kept building too. By May 2026 new EV sales had climbed to 21,303 units with battery electrics hitting a record 20 percent market share

Used EV sales more than doubled in a single month, jumping from 3,176 units in February to 7,557 in March, according to the <a href="https://zecar.com/reviews/used-ev-sales-doubled-march-2026-australia" style="color:`#a8ff78`;border-bottom:1px solid rgba(168,255,120,0.3);">Australian Automotive Dealer Association</a>. Stock dropped 38 percent at the same time. Days of supply fell to 28.6 days. A balanced market normally sits between 60 and 90 days. Used EVs were selling faster than dealers could source them.

**The real problem isn't today's petrol price**

It's not knowing what petrol will cost next month.

One month it's $1.70 per litre. A few weeks later it's pushing past $2.50. Global conflicts, refinery outages and shipping disruptions can all affect what you pay at the pump. Australia imports around 90 percent of its refined fuel, according to <a href="https://www.ibtimes.com.au/australia-fuel-crisis-deepens-april-2026-diesel-shortages-hit-farms-trucks-recession-warnings-1866595" style="color:`#a8ff78`;border-bottom:1px solid rgba(168,255,120,0.3);">Australian government energy statistics</a>, which means when global supply gets squeezed, we feel it fast and there is not a lot anyone can do about it in the short term.

That uncertainty is becoming one of the strongest arguments for switching to an EV.

Electricity prices can change too, but they are generally far more predictable than global oil markets. And if you have rooftop solar, you are even more insulated from price swings. An EV charged at home from your own solar panels is about as close to energy independence as most Australians are realistically going to get. No car makes you completely immune to energy markets, but an EV gets you a lot closer than a petrol car does.

**Australians are doing the maths**

The biggest reason EV adoption is growing isn't because people suddenly became car enthusiasts. It's because the numbers have genuinely improved.

A few years ago buyers faced limited choice, long wait times and higher purchase prices. Today there are dozens of EV models available across multiple price points, Chinese brands have created serious competition on pricing, and the FBT exemption has made novated leasing one of the most tax-effective ways to buy a car in Australia.

For many households the financial gap between an EV and a petrol car has never been smaller.

The important thing is not to focus only on the drive-away price. The real story is in the total cost of ownership.

**Is now a good time to buy?**

Supply has improved significantly since the peak of the crisis. The largest ever single dedicated new-energy vehicle shipment docked in Melbourne earlier this month, with the custom-built car carrier BYD Zhengzhou unloading nearly 5,000 vehicles direct from Shanghai to satisfy backlogged orders, <a href="https://www.thenewdaily.com.au/life/auto/2026/03/18/ev-interest-surges" style="color:`#a8ff78`;border-bottom:1px solid rgba(168,255,120,0.3);">as reported by The New Daily</a>.

EOFY deals are running until June 30 across BYD, Tesla, Toyota, Renault and others. The FBT exemption is still intact for eligible EVs under $91,387, with a government review flagged for mid-2027. If you are driving long distances, spending heavily on fuel, or have access to salary packaging through your employer, the numbers can be surprisingly compelling right now.

**The bottom line**

Australians are not buying EVs because they suddenly fell in love with electric cars. They are buying them because petrol got expensive, stations ran dry, and the long-term ownership costs finally make sense for a lot more households than they used to.

The fuel crisis will ease. The shift it triggered almost certainly won't.
