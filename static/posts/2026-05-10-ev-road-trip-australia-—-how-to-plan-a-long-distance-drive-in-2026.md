One of the most common concerns people have before buying an EV is whether it can handle a long road trip in Australia. The distances are real, the charging network is still developing, and the last thing you want is to be stranded on the Hume Highway with 5% battery.

The good news is that EV road trips in Australia are genuinely doable in 2026 — and with a bit of planning, they're stress-free. Here's exactly how to do it.

- - -

### How EV Road Trips Are Different

Driving an EV long distance isn't harder than driving a petrol car — it's just different. Instead of stopping whenever you feel like it for a 3-minute fuel top-up, you plan your stops in advance and charge for 20-30 minutes at a time while you grab coffee or food.

Most experienced EV drivers actually enjoy the enforced breaks. Long highway drives are tiring and regular stops improve alertness. The difference is you're choosing where to stop based on charging locations rather than wherever a servo appears.

The key is planning ahead rather than winging it. Here's how.

- - -

### Step 1 — Know Your Car's Real-World Range

WLTP range figures are tested under ideal conditions. Real-world highway range is typically 15-25% lower due to higher speeds, air conditioning, and load.

Here's a rough guide for popular Australian EVs at highway speeds:

**Tesla Model 3 Long Range** — rated 629km WLTP, expect 480-530km real-world highway range

**Tesla Model Y Long Range** — rated 533km WLTP, expect 400-460km real-world highway range

**BYD Seal Long Range** — rated 570km WLTP, expect 430-480km real-world highway range

**Hyundai Ioniq 6 Long Range** — rated 614km WLTP, expect 470-520km real-world highway range

**BYD Atto 3** — rated 420km WLTP, expect 310-360km real-world highway range

**MG4** — rated 435km WLTP, expect 320-370km real-world highway range

As a rule of thumb, plan your charging stops based on 80% of the WLTP figure to stay comfortable. Never plan to arrive at a charger below 10-15% — always leave a buffer.

- - -

### Step 2 — Plan Your Route and Charging Stops

This is the most important step and it takes about 10 minutes with the right tools.

#### Apps and tools to use

**PlugShare** — the best crowd-sourced charging map in Australia. Shows every charger type, real-time availability, and user check-ins with comments about reliability. Essential for any EV road trip.

**ABRP (A Better Route Planner)** — purpose-built EV route planner. Enter your car model, current battery level, and destination and it calculates exactly where to stop and for how long. Accounts for elevation, speed, and weather. Free and genuinely excellent.

**Tesla Navigation** — if you drive a Tesla, the built-in navigation automatically routes you through Superchargers and pre-conditions the battery before arrival. The gold standard for road trip planning.

**Chargefox app** — shows Chargefox network locations across Australia, real-time availability, and lets you start charging sessions.

#### The general rule for stop planning

Plan to charge to around 80% at each stop rather than 100% — charging slows significantly above 80% and it's faster to make an extra stop than wait for the last 20%. Most fast chargers can get you from 20% to 80% in 20-30 minutes depending on your car and charger speed.

- - -

### Step 3 — Know the Charging Networks

Australia has several charging networks and knowing which ones your car uses makes life easier.

**Tesla Supercharger** — Australia's best fast charging network. Over 100 locations, reliable, fast, and integrated into Tesla navigation. Now open to non-Tesla vehicles at most locations via the Tesla app.

**Chargefox** — Australia's largest third-party network. Good coverage on major highways particularly on the east coast. Ultra-rapid chargers at key locations.

**Evie Networks** — growing network with a focus on highway corridors. Reliable and well-maintained.

**BP Pulse** — BP servo locations with fast chargers. Convenient because you can grab food and coffee at the same stop.

**NRMA** — strong coverage in NSW and ACT particularly in regional areas where other networks are thin.

For most east coast routes the coverage is now good enough that range anxiety is rarely justified with a modern long-range EV. Regional and outback routes still require more careful planning.

#### Outback and remote routes

These require serious planning and in some cases a support strategy. The Nullarbor has improved significantly with charging stations added in recent years but gaps still exist. Research specifically for your car's range before attempting remote routes.

### Tips for a Stress-Free EV Road Trip

**Pre-condition your battery before fast charging** — most EVs let you set a destination charger in the navigation which automatically warms the battery for faster charging. Use it.

**Charge to 80% at each stop, not 100%** — faster overall journey time due to charging speed tapering above 80%.

**Use cruise control** — consistent highway speeds are significantly more efficient than variable speeds. Set it to the speed limit and let the car manage consumption.

**Reduce air con where practical** — air conditioning is one of the biggest range killers on highway drives. Even turning it down slightly makes a meaningful difference on hot days.

**Download offline maps** — mobile coverage in regional Australia can be patchy. Download your route offline in Google Maps or Apple Maps before you leave.

**Check charger status before you leave each stop** — use PlugShare to confirm your next charger is operational before you drive away with a half-full battery.

**Carry a Type 2 cable** — some chargers are tethered but others require you to bring your own cable. Most EVs come with one but confirm before you leave.

- - -

### What If a Charger Is Broken or Occupied?

This is the most common concern and the honest answer is it does happen occasionally. Here's how to handle it:

Always have a backup charger identified — PlugShare shows alternatives nearby. Never arrive at a charger with less than 10-15% battery if you're in a regional area with limited alternatives.

If a charger is occupied, most modern EVs have enough range to wait or find an alternative without stress — as long as you haven't planned too tightly.

The charging network reliability is improving rapidly and the days of regularly broken chargers are largely behind us on major routes. Regional areas are still less consistent.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Can you do a road trip in an EV in Australia?**

Yes — major highway routes on the east coast are well covered in 2026. Regional and remote routes require more planning but are increasingly doable with modern long-range EVs.

**How long do EV charging stops take on a road trip?**

20-30 minutes at a DC fast charger to go from around 20% to 80%. Most drivers use this time for food, coffee, or a bathroom break.

**Which EV is best for road trips in Australia?**

Tesla Model 3 and Model Y have the best charging network access via Superchargers. The Hyundai Ioniq 6 has the fastest charging speed of any non-Tesla. Both are excellent road trip cars.

**How do I find EV chargers in Australia?**

PlugShare and ABRP are the best tools. The Chargefox and Evie apps show their own networks. Tesla navigation handles routing automatically for Tesla owners.

**Is the charging network good enough for road trips in Australia?**

On major east coast routes — yes. Melbourne to Sydney, Brisbane to Sydney, and Perth to Margaret River are all well covered. Remote outback routes still require careful planning.
