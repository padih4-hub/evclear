You've heard it a hundred times — EVs are cheaper to run than petrol cars. But what does that actually mean in dollars for an Australian driver in 2026?

Let's stop talking in generalities and look at the real numbers.

#### The fuel cost comparison

This is where the biggest saving comes from. Let's use realistic Australian averages:

**Petrol car:**

* Fuel consumption: 11L per 100km (average SUV/hatch)
* Petrol price: $2.00 per litre
* Cost per 100km: $22.00

**Electric car:**

* Energy consumption: 18kWh per 100km (average EV)
* Electricity rate: $0.30 per kWh (standard home tariff)
* Cost per 100km: $5.40

That's a saving of **$16.60 every 100km.**

Drive 15,000km per year — the Australian average — and you're saving **$2,490 annually** on fuel alone.

Drive 20,000km per year and that saving jumps to **$3,320 per year.**

#### What if you have solar?

If you have rooftop solar and charge during the day using excess solar energy, your effective charging cost can drop to as low as $0.05 to $0.10 per kWh — essentially free electricity.

At $0.08 per kWh the cost per 100km drops to just $1.44. Against a petrol car's $22.00 per 100km, that's a saving of over $3,000 per year on 15,000km.

Many Australian EV owners with solar report their total annual energy bill actually decreases after buying an EV — because the solar system that was previously exporting cheap excess energy to the grid is now charging their car instead.

#### The off-peak advantage

Even without solar, switching to an off-peak electricity tariff for overnight charging can make a significant difference. Many Australian energy retailers offer rates as low as $0.15 to $0.22 per kWh during off-peak hours — typically 11pm to 6am.

At $0.18 per kWh your cost per 100km drops to $3.24. That's an annual saving of $2,814 compared to petrol on 15,000km — nearly $240 per month.

The 30 minutes it takes to switch your electricity plan is one of the highest ROI activities you can do after buying an EV.

#### Servicing costs

This is the saving most people forget to include — and it's significant.

Petrol cars have hundreds of moving parts that wear out and need replacing. Oil changes, timing belts, spark plugs, exhaust systems, transmission fluid, radiator coolant. The list goes on.

EVs have dramatically fewer moving parts. No oil changes. No timing belts. No exhaust. Brake pads last significantly longer because regenerative braking does most of the work.

**Typical annual servicing costs:**

* Petrol car: $800 to $1,200 per year
* Electric car: $300 to $600 per year

Over 5 years that's a saving of $2,500 to $4,500 in servicing costs alone.

#### The 5 year total cost comparison

Let's put it all together. Here's a real comparison between a $39,990 BYD Atto 3 and a similarly priced $39,990 petrol SUV, driving 15,000km annually for 5 years:

| Cost                     | BYD Atto 3  | Petrol SUV  |
| ------------------------ | ----------- | ----------- |
| Purchase price           | $39,990     | $39,990     |
| Fuel / electricity (5yr) | $4,050      | $16,500     |
| Servicing (5yr)          | $3,000      | $5,500      |
| **Total**                | **$47,040** | **$61,990** |

**Total 5-year saving: $14,950**

On a car that costs exactly the same to buy.

#### What about depreciation?

Depreciation is the one area where EVs have historically been less predictable than petrol cars. The rapid pace of EV development means older models can lose value faster as newer, longer-range models arrive at lower prices.

That said, the most popular EV brands — Tesla, Kia, BYD — are showing increasingly strong resale values in Australia as the used EV market matures. The Kia EV3 and Tesla Model Y in particular are holding value well.

The honest answer is that depreciation varies significantly by brand and model. Research the specific car you're considering on Carsales before buying.

#### The registration and stamp duty saving

Depending on your state, you may also save on stamp duty and registration when buying an EV. In NSW, EVs under $78,000 are exempt from stamp duty — a saving of up to $3,000 on a $40,000 car. The ACT offers stamp duty exemptions plus two years free registration.

These vary by state and change frequently — always check your state government's current scheme before buying.

#### When do you actually break even?

The breakeven point — the moment your total cost of EV ownership drops below what you'd have spent on a petrol car — depends on your specific situation. Key variables are how much you drive, your electricity rate, and the price difference between the two cars.

For most Australian drivers buying a comparably priced EV and charging at home, the breakeven point falls somewhere between 2 and 4 years. After that, every kilometre you drive is saving you money.

#### The honest bottom line

The "EVs are cheaper to run" claim isn't marketing spin — the numbers are real and they're significant. For the average Australian driver the total saving over 5 years of EV ownership ranges from $10,000 to $20,000 depending on how much you drive and how you charge.

The upfront cost gap that used to make EVs hard to justify is largely gone in 2026. When the purchase price is the same and the running costs are dramatically lower — the financial case for going electric has never been stronger.
