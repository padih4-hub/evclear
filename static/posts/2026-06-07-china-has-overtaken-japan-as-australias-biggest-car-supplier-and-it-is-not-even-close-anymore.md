Something happened in February 2026 that nobody expected to see quite so soon.

China overtook Japan as Australia's biggest source of new vehicles. For the first time since 1998, Japan was not the top supplier of cars to this country. A title it had held for 28 years, gone in a single month.

And by April, it wasn't even close anymore.

Almost 36,000 passenger cars from China arrived in Australia in April 2026, well ahead of the 29,000 from Japan. In the first four months of 2026 alone, more than 100,000 Chinese-built vehicles landed here. That's a 51 percent increase on the same period last year.

China's numbers were up 45 percent year on year. Japan's were down 31 percent. That's not a gradual shift, that's a dramatic realignment happening in real time.

**What is actually driving it**

The short answer is electric vehicles and fuel prices.

More than 40,000 of the passenger vehicles arriving from China in the first four months of 2026 were EVs. Chinese manufacturers have built a commanding lead in EV technology and production volume, and they are now exporting that advantage aggressively into markets like Australia that have no trade barriers on Chinese vehicles.

The Middle East conflict-driven fuel price spike has accelerated things further. When petrol gets expensive and stays expensive, buyers start doing the maths on running costs. Chinese EV brands happen to be sitting on the most affordable entry points in the market right now.

BYD was the second biggest selling brand in Australia in May 2026, up 154.6 percent year on year. Geely was up 416 percent. Omoda Jaecoo was up 729 percent. Those are not rounding errors. Those are brands that barely existed in the Australian market 18 months ago now competing directly with Toyota, Ford and Hyundai.

**What it means if you are buying an EV right now**

For buyers, this competition is genuinely good news.

The influx of Chinese brands has forced established manufacturers to respond. Hyundai just permanently cut $8,000 off the Kona Electric and IONIQ 5 directly because of competitive pressure from Chinese brands. MG, Nissan and Toyota have all sharpened their EOFY pricing for the same reason.

Industry analysts are predicting Chinese brands could account for over 40 percent of Australia's new vehicle market by 2030 if current growth trends hold. That projection may end up being conservative.

For buyers, more competition means more choice and lower prices. The EV market in Australia in mid-2026 has more options at more price points than at any time in history. The Jaecoo J5 EV at $35,990, the BYD Atto 2 at $31,990, the Geely EX5 at a competitive mid-size price point, and now a properly priced Hyundai Kona Electric from $46,000.

If you have been waiting for the right time to look seriously at an EV, the market is arguably better positioned for buyers right now than it has ever been.

**What happens next**

More Chinese brands are coming. XPeng just became operational in Australia as a factory importer. Firefly, a sub-brand of Nio, is in active discussions with local distributors. Jetour has invited Australian media to pre-launch events this month. Forthing starts selling the Taikon 5 from June 2026.

The pace of new entrants is not slowing. If anything it is accelerating.

For legacy brands, the pressure will only increase. For Australian buyers, the beneficiaries of all this competition are you.

**The bottom line**

Japan held the top spot in Australian car imports for 28 years. China took it in a single month and has not looked back. The driver is EVs, affordable pricing and aggressive market entry from brands most Australians had never heard of two years ago.

The Australian car market is being reshaped faster than almost anyone predicted. The good news is that buyers are the ones winning.
