If you've just bought an EV or you're about to, a home charger is the first thing you should sort out. Relying on a standard power point works in a pinch but a dedicated wall charger makes EV ownership genuinely effortless — you plug in when you get home and wake up to a full car every morning.

The problem is the market is full of cheap imports and overcomplicated specs. Here's the honest guide to what actually matters and what's worth buying in Australia right now.

- - -

### Do You Actually Need a Home Charger?

Short answer — yes, if you can install one.

Every EV comes with a cable that plugs into a standard 10A power point. It works but it's slow — typically adding 10-15km of range per hour. For most Australians driving under 80km a day, that's actually fine overnight.

But a dedicated 7kW wall charger adds around 40-50km of range per hour. Most EVs go from near empty to full in 4-6 hours. It's faster, safer, and more convenient — and the installation cost pays itself back quickly in peace of mind alone.

If you live in an apartment without a dedicated car space, a home charger isn't an option yet — you'll need to rely on public charging for now.

- - -

### What to Look for in a Home EV Charger

#### Power output — 7kW is the sweet spot

Home chargers in Australia typically come in 7kW or 22kW. For most households, 7kW is more than enough — it'll fully charge any current EV overnight on a standard single-phase connection.

22kW chargers require three-phase power which most Australian homes don't have. Unless you know you have three-phase, assume you need a 7kW charger.

#### Australian certification

This is non-negotiable. Your charger must be certified to Australian standards — look for SAA approval or compliance with AS/NZS 3000. Cheap uncertified imports are a genuine fire risk and won't be covered by your home insurance.

#### Type 2 connector

Every modern EV sold in Australia uses a Type 2 connector for AC charging. Make sure your charger has a Type 2 plug or socket — this is standard on all quality chargers but worth confirming.

#### Smart features

A smart charger lets you schedule charging for off-peak electricity rates, monitor your energy usage, and in some cases integrate with solar. If you have solar or a time-of-use tariff, smart charging can save you hundreds per year.

#### Warranty

Look for at least 2-3 years warranty. Quality chargers from established brands carry 3 years as standard.

### What to Avoid

**Unbranded cheap imports** — anything without Australian certification is a genuine risk. Not worth saving $200 on a device that handles high current 24/7.

**22kW chargers for standard homes** — pointless if you don't have three-phase power, which most Australian homes don't.

**Buying without checking your switchboard** — older homes may need a switchboard upgrade before a 7kW charger can be installed. Get an electrician to check before you buy.

### How Much Does Installation Cost?

Installation by a licensed electrician typically runs $500 to $1,500 depending on:

* Distance from switchboard to garage
* Whether your switchboard needs upgrading
* Your state and the electrician

Total cost including charger and installation: roughly $1,500 to $2,500 for most Australian homes. On a novated lease, the electricity costs from charging can be bundled as a pre-tax expense — the charger hardware itself cannot, but it's still a one-time cost that pays back quickly in convenience and charging speed.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a home charger for an EV in Australia?**

Not strictly — the included cable and a standard power point will work for most daily driving. But a 7kW wall charger is significantly more convenient and worth installing if you have a garage or driveway.

**What is the best home EV charger in Australia?**

For most people the EVSE Australia home series or Ocular IQ are the standout options. Solar households should look at the Ocular IQ or Zappi for solar diversion capability.

**How long does a home EV charger take to install?**

A licensed electrician can typically install a home charger in 2-4 hours. Add time if your switchboard needs upgrading.

**Can I install a home EV charger myself?**

No — it must be installed by a licensed electrician in Australia. DIY installation is illegal and voids your warranty and home insurance.

**Will a home charger increase my electricity bill?**

Yes but the increase is offset by eliminating fuel costs. Most EV owners find their total energy bill goes up modestly while their overall transport costs drop significantly.
