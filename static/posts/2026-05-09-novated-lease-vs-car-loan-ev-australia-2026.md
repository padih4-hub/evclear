If you're buying an EV in Australia and trying to figure out whether a novated lease or a car loan makes more financial sense, you're asking the right question. The answer isn't always obvious — and most comparison articles either gloss over the numbers or have a vested interest in pointing you one way.

Here's the honest breakdown of novated lease vs car loan for an EV in Australia in 2026, with real numbers.

- - -

**What's the Actual Difference?**

A car loan is straightforward. You borrow money, buy the car, make repayments with after-tax income, and own it outright at the end.

A novated lease is more complex. Your employer leases the car on your behalf and the repayments — plus running costs like rego, insurance, servicing, and charging — come out of your pre-tax salary. That lowers your taxable income. And because eligible EVs are currently exempt from Fringe Benefits Tax, you get the full tax benefit with none of the usual FBT cost.

That difference in how payments are structured is what makes the comparison so stark.

- - -

**The Numbers Side by Side**

Let's use a real example so this isn't abstract.

You earn $100,000 a year. You want a $62,000 BYD Seal Premium. You're looking at a 3-year term.

**Car Loan**

* Borrowing $62,000 at roughly 7% interest over 3 years
* Monthly repayment: approximately $1,915
* Total repaid over 3 years: approximately $68,940
* All repayments come from after-tax income
* You own the car at the end

**Novated Lease**

* Same car, same term
* Lease repayments come from pre-tax salary
* Running costs — rego, insurance, servicing, charging — bundled in pre-tax
* Zero FBT on eligible EV
* Estimated total tax saving over 3 years: $13,000 to $18,000
* Residual payment at end if you want to own it outright

The novated lease wins on pure numbers — by a significant margin — for most employees above $70,000. The tax saving is simply too large to ignore.

**Where the Car Loan Wins**

It's not all one-sided. A car loan makes more sense in a few situations:

**You're self-employed or a contractor.** Novated leases are an employee benefit. If you don't have an employer offering salary packaging, a car loan is your only real option.

**Your employer doesn't offer salary packaging.** Same issue — no salary packaging, no novated lease. Ask HR first before you assume.

**You want full ownership flexibility.** With a car loan you own the car. You can sell it, modify it, or pay it off early without dealing with a lease structure or a residual payment.

**You're close to retirement or planning a career change.** If you're not going to be in stable employment for the full lease term, a car loan is simpler and less risky.

**Your income is under $70,000.** The tax saving on a novated lease is real but smaller at lower income brackets. It still usually wins — but the gap narrows.

- - -

**The Hidden Costs of a Novated Lease**

This is what most novated lease providers won't put front and centre.

**Provider fees.** Novated lease providers charge management fees — sometimes bundled into the repayment, sometimes not. These can be $500 to $1,500 per year depending on the provider. Always ask for a full fee breakdown before signing.

**Residual payment.** At the end of the lease you typically owe a residual — a lump sum to own the car outright. This is set by the ATO as a percentage of the vehicle's original value. On a $62,000 car over 3 years, that's roughly $19,220. You can pay it, refinance it, or hand the car back.

**Locked into employment.** If you leave your employer mid-lease and your new employer doesn't offer salary packaging, the lease reverts to you personally. You keep the car but lose the pre-tax benefit for the remainder of the term.

**Reportable fringe benefits.** Even though the EV is FBT-exempt, it still shows up on your income statement. That can affect HECS repayments, Medicare Levy Surcharge thresholds, and Centrelink eligibility. Talk to an accountant if any of those apply.

**What About the FBT Changes?**

The government has confirmed the EV FBT exemption is being wound back in phases:

Until March 2027 — full exemption for all eligible EVs under the LCT threshold. Nothing changes yet.

April 2027 to March 2029 — EVs under $75k keep the full exemption. EVs between $75k and the LCT threshold drop to a 25% discount only.

From April 2029 — full exemption ends. All eligible EVs move to a 25% discount permanently.

This doesn't change the comparison dramatically for EVs under $75k right now — but it does mean the window for maximum savings is open today, not indefinitely. Existing leases are expected to be grandfathered under the rules that applied when they were signed.

**So Which One Should You Choose?**

For most Australian employees earning above $70,000 with access to salary packaging — the novated lease wins. The tax saving over 3 years is typically $13,000 to $18,000 on a mid-range EV. A car loan can't compete with that mathematically.

But if you're self-employed, your employer doesn't offer salary packaging, or your employment situation is uncertain — a car loan keeps things simpler and gives you full ownership from day one.
