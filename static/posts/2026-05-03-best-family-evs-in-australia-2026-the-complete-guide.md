Buying a family EV in Australia used to mean one or two options at eye-watering prices. In 2026 that's changed completely. You can now get a genuine 7-seat electric SUV from $97,000, a roomy 5-seat family hauler from under $60,000, and a premium people mover from $106,000.

The problem isn't choice anymore. It's knowing which one actually works for Australian families — school runs, weekend trips, the occasional Bunnings run, and the odd interstate drive.

This guide cuts through the noise. We've focused on EVs that are actually on sale in Australia right now, with real pricing, real range, and honest takes on who each car suits.

### What Makes a Good Family EV?

Before we get into the cars, here's what we're judging on:

**Space** — does it genuinely fit a family? A "7-seat" car where two of those seats are unusable doesn't count.

**Range** — can you do a weekend road trip without stopping every two hours? We look for 400km+ real-world range for family use.

**Charging speed** — faster DC charging matters when you're travelling with kids and need to top up quickly.

**Running costs** — the whole point of switching to electric is saving money. We factor in fuel savings, servicing, and state incentives.

**Value** — what do you actually get for the money?

### The Best Family EVs in Australia Right Now

#### 1. Kia EV9 — Best 7-Seat Family EV Overall

**Price:** From $97,000 (Air) to $129,250 (GT) **Range:** 443km (Air) to 512km (Earth) **Seats:** 6 or 7

The EV9 is the family EV most Australians have been waiting for. It's the first electric car that genuinely solves the 7-seat problem — not by cramming two tiny fold-up seats into the boot, but by designing a proper three-row vehicle from the ground up.

The third row is actually usable for adults on shorter trips. The second row reclines and has powered leg rests on higher grades. Boot space with all seats up is generous. The 800V charging architecture means you can add 100km of range in around 15 minutes at a fast charger.

The Air grade at $97,000 is the one to buy for most families. The Earth at $106,500 adds the longer-range 100kWh battery. The GT is a performance machine that most families won't need.

**Best for:** Families who genuinely need 7 seats and do regular road trips. **Watch out for:** Price. At $97,000+ it's not cheap, though the running costs help close the gap over time.

#### 2. Hyundai Ioniq 9 — Best Premium Family SUV

**Price:** From $119,750 **Range:** Up to 600km **Seats:** 6 or 7

The Ioniq 9 is Hyundai's answer to the EV9 and it's impressive. Built on the same E-GMP platform as the Ioniq 5 and 6, it brings a longer wheelbase, three rows, and some of the best interior space in any electric vehicle sold in Australia.

The 110kWh battery delivers up to 600km WLTP range — genuinely useful for interstate family travel. The interior is wide, quiet, and thoughtfully laid out with storage everywhere.

At $119,750 it sits above the EV9 Air but below the EV9 Earth. For families who prioritise range over price, it's a serious contender.

**Best for:** Large families who want maximum range and premium interior quality. **Watch out for:** Higher entry price than the EV9. Only one grade currently available in Australia.

#### 3. Volvo EX90 — Best Premium 7-Seat EV

**Price:** From $124,990 (Plus) to $134,990 (Ultra) **Range:** Up to 570km **Seats:** 6 or 7

The EX90 is Volvo's flagship electric SUV and it shows. The safety technology is class-leading — it's the first production car with a driver monitoring system that can detect drowsiness and distraction and intervene. For families driving long distances, that matters.

The interior is beautifully simple. Scandinavian design, premium materials, and a Google-powered infotainment system that actually works. The third row is best suited to children rather than adults, but the second row is excellent.

The 111kWh battery and up to 570km WLTP range means genuine all-day capability without range anxiety.

**Best for:** Families who want safety, premium quality, and a European badge. **Watch out for:** Third row is tight for adults. Price puts it above the EV9 and Ioniq 9.

#### 4. Volkswagen ID.Buzz — Best Family People Mover

**Price:** From $75,990 (Pro SWB) to $104,990 (GTX LWB) **Range:** Up to 487km **Seats:** 5 (SWB) or 7 (LWB)

If you need maximum interior space rather than off-road capability, the ID.Buzz is unlike anything else on the market. Based on the iconic Kombi, it has a completely flat floor, sliding rear doors, and genuinely excellent space utilisation.

The Long Wheelbase version seats 7 with the third row actually being comfortable for adults. The 91kWh battery delivers up to 487km WLTP range in the LWB.

At $80,990 for the Pro LWB, it's better value than many people expect.

**Best for:** Families who do school runs, sports transport, and road trips where cabin space matters more than ground clearance. **Watch out for:** No all-wheel drive on the Pro grades. The retro styling isn't for everyone.

### What About Charging on Family Road Trips?

This is the question every family asks. The honest answer is that charging on road trips is manageable but requires planning.

For vehicles with 800V charging (EV9, Ioniq 5, Ioniq 9), a 15-20 minute stop at a fast charger adds 100km+ of range — roughly the time it takes for a toilet break and a coffee. The 350kW charger network in Australia is still growing but is now adequate on major highways between capital cities.

For 400V vehicles (ID.Buzz, Mifa 9), expect 30-45 minutes for a meaningful top-up. Plan your stops around lunch breaks rather than quick pitstops.

### State Incentives for Family EV Buyers

Most state cash rebates have closed, but the federal FBT exemption via novated lease is still the biggest financial incentive available to Australian families.

If you're an employee and your employer offers salary packaging, buying your family EV through a novated lease can save $5,000–$15,000+ depending on your income. The EV must be priced under $91,387 at first retail sale to qualify — which rules out the EV9 GT, Ioniq 9, Volvo EX90, and most Porsche and BMW variants.

The Kia EV5, Ioniq 5 base, ID.Buzz Pro SWB, and LDV Mifa 9 Mode all qualify for the FBT exemption.

### The Bottom Line

For most Australian families, the Kia EV9 is the answer — it's the only vehicle that genuinely solves the 7-seat problem without major compromises. The Ioniq 9 is excellent if range is your priority. The ID.Buzz is the pick if you want people mover practicality at a more accessible price.

If you have 2-4 kids and don't need that third row, the Ioniq 5 or Kia EV5 will serve you better at a lower price — with lower running costs to match.

The family EV market in 2026 is genuinely good. The question isn't whether there's an option for your family — it's which one.
