Australia's EV market just hit a milestone. In April 2026, electric vehicles accounted for 16.4% of all new car sales — nearly one in six cars sold. That's the highest market share EVs have ever achieved in Australia, and it's changing which cars are actually moving off dealer floors.

Here's the breakdown of what Australians are actually buying right now — and why some of the results might surprise you.

- - -

### Why April Was a Record Month

The fuel price crisis has been the catalyst. With petrol hitting $2.20-$2.45 per litre in many parts of Australia due to global supply disruption, buyers who had been sitting on the fence have made the decision. The running cost advantage of an EV has never been more visible at the bowser.

Combined with the lowest EV prices Australia has ever seen and the FBT exemption still fully intact, April saw a surge in EV purchases that pushed market share to record levels.

### The BYD Sealion 7 — Australia's Surprise Hit

The Sealion 7 wasn't on most people's radar 12 months ago. Now it's the best selling EV in Australia and one of the top 10 best selling cars overall — seventh in April regardless of powertrain.

Sales are up 342% year to date. That's not a marketing spike — it's genuine sustained demand from buyers who have done the maths and found the Sealion 7 sits in a sweet spot of price, range, and practicality that nothing else quite matches at the same price point.

At $54,990 for the Premium it sits comfortably under both the $75k FBT threshold and the $91,387 LCT threshold — meaning it qualifies for full FBT exemption on a novated lease well beyond April 2027. For employees with salary packaging access, that's a meaningful advantage.

- - -

### Tesla Model Y — Still Dominant But Facing Real Competition

The Model Y remains a juggernaut. April saw 973 sales in a single month — its highest ever in Australia. The refreshed Model Y has helped, and the fuel price crisis has pushed buyers who were considering a petrol SUV to reconsider.

But Tesla's dominance is no longer unchallenged. The Sealion 7, Zeekr 7X, and Kia EV5 are all eating into territory that used to be exclusively Tesla's. The main advantages Tesla retains are the Supercharger network for long distance driving and software depth — both still meaningful differentiators for buyers who do regular interstate trips.

- - -

### The Zeekr 7X — The Fast Charger That Surprised Everyone

Zeekr is a brand most Australians had never heard of 18 months ago. Now the 7X is averaging 675 sales per month — consistently.

The reason is straightforward: the 7X has one of the fastest charging speeds of any EV in Australia at up to 360kW DC. At that speed it can add hundreds of kilometres of range in minutes rather than the 20-30 minutes most competitors require. For buyers who care about charging speed — particularly those without easy home charging — that's a genuinely compelling differentiator.

At $57,900 for the RWD it also sits comfortably under the FBT thresholds with strong range and a premium interior that punches above its price point.

- - -

### Kia's Quiet Surge

Kia doesn't make as much noise as BYD or Tesla but the numbers are strong. The EV3 is up 150% year to date — the most popular small electric SUV in its price range. Kia's advantage is brand familiarity. For buyers nervous about committing to a Chinese brand they've never heard of, a Kia EV feels like a safer bet even if the specs are comparable.

The EV3 starts from $47,600 and offers up to 604km of WLTP range on the long range variant — which is exceptional at the price point. For someone who wants a genuinely capable EV from a brand with an established Australian dealer network, the EV3 is hard to argue with.

- - -

### Toyota Finally Has an EV People Are Buying

The bZ4X had a rough start in Australia. Low sales, limited model range, and a brand that seemed uncertain about EVs. In 2026 that's changed.

The bZ4X is averaging 330 sales per month in 2026 after averaging less than 87 per month in 2025. Fuel prices have pushed Toyota loyalists to finally give it a proper look — and the 2026 model with a meaningful price cut and improved range has given them a reason to say yes.

It won't trouble the Sealion 7 or Model Y at the top of the charts, but Toyota having a credible EV that's actually selling is good for the whole market.
