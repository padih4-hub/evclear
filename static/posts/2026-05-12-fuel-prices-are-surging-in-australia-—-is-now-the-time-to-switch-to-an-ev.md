If you've filled up a petrol car recently, you've felt it. Fuel prices in Australia have surged significantly in 2026, driven by ongoing global supply disruption, and it's changing the way Australians think about their next car purchase.

EV interest has hit record levels as a result. The question is whether switching to an EV right now actually makes financial sense — or whether it's just an emotional reaction to a bad few weeks at the bowser.

Here's the honest answer.

- - -

### What's Happening With Fuel Prices

Global fuel supply has been disrupted by ongoing geopolitical tensions, pushing petrol prices at Australian pumps to levels that are making the daily commute genuinely painful for many households.

The impact on EV interest has been immediate and dramatic. EV searches on Australia's largest car marketplace have nearly tripled compared to earlier in the year, with BYD, Tesla, and newer Chinese brands seeing the biggest spikes in interest.

This isn't just curiosity — it's people doing the maths on their commuting costs and realising the numbers look very different at $2.20+ per litre than they did at $1.80.

### Is This the Right Time to Buy?

The honest answer is that the best time to buy an EV has never been about waiting for the perfect moment — it's about whether the numbers work for your situation. But right now, several factors are aligning simultaneously:

**Fuel prices are high** — making the running cost saving larger than it's been in years

**EV prices are the lowest they've ever been** — with models now starting from under $25,000

**The FBT exemption is still fully intact** — but being wound back from April 2027 for EVs over $75k and from April 2029 for everyone

**The model range is the strongest it's ever been** — 249+ models across every price point and vehicle category

The risk of acting now is buying the wrong car in a rush. The risk of waiting is continuing to pay high fuel costs and narrowing the FBT window.

### What to Watch Out For When Buying in a Hurry

High demand periods are when dealers get sloppy — or worse, opportunistic. A few things to watch:

**Don't pay above list price** — EV supply has improved significantly. If a dealer is asking above the manufacturer's recommended price, walk away.

**Check delivery timelines** — some popular models have wait times of 2-4 months. Factor that into your decision if you want to stop paying for petrol quickly.

**Don't skip the novated lease calculation** — if your employer offers salary packaging, the FBT exemption on a novated lease can save $13,000-$25,000 over 3 years. That saving exists regardless of what petrol costs. Read the [complete novated lease guide on EVClear](https://evclear.com.au/posts/2026-05-04-novated-lease-ev-australia-2026-how-it-works-what-you-save-and-what-to-watch-out-for) before you go to a dealer.

**Sort your home charging first** — the running cost saving assumes home charging at roughly 30c/kWh. If you're relying entirely on public DC fast chargers, the saving is significantly smaller. Have a plan for home charging before you commit.

### The Breakeven Question

One of the most common questions when fuel prices spike is whether the payback period shortens enough to justify buying now versus waiting.

At $2.20 per litre, the answer is yes — meaningfully so for most buyers.

A mid-range EV like the BYD Seal at $52,990 versus a comparable petrol SUV at around $45,000 has roughly a $8,000 price premium. At $2,490 annual fuel saving (15,000km/year), that premium pays back in around 3.2 years — before you add servicing savings or novated lease benefits.

Add the novated lease FBT saving and the breakeven drops to under 12 months for most employees on decent salaries.
