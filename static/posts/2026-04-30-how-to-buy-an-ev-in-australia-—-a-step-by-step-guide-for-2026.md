Buying an EV in Australia in 2026 is genuinely exciting. The market has never had more options, prices have never been more competitive, and the ownership experience has never been better.

But the buying process has a few unique wrinkles compared to buying a petrol car. If you go in unprepared, those wrinkles can cost you thousands.

Here's the complete step-by-step guide to buying an EV in Australia — from first research to driving away.

#### Step 1 — Work out if an EV suits your life

Before you look at a single model, be honest about your situation.

Do you have somewhere to charge at home? A garage, driveway, or dedicated parking spot makes EV ownership seamless. Without home charging, you'll be relying on public chargers for everything — which is workable but adds friction.

How far do you drive daily? The average Australian drives under 50km per day. Even the most affordable EVs handle that easily. If you regularly drive 200km+ days through regional areas, you need to think more carefully about range and charging infrastructure.

Are you thinking long term? EVs cost more upfront in some cases but significantly less to run. If you're keeping the car for 3 to 5 years or more, the total cost of ownership almost always favours the EV.

#### Step 2 — Set your real budget

Your budget isn't just the purchase price. Factor in:

**Drive-away price** — always compare drive-away, not the advertised price. Drive-away includes registration, stamp duty, CTP insurance, and dealer delivery. A car advertised at $45,000 might cost $49,500 drive-away in Queensland.

**Home charger installation** — budget $1,000 to $1,500 for a 7kW wallbox unless your dealer includes one. Always ask before assuming.

**Electricity plan** — factor in switching to an EV-friendly tariff with cheap off-peak rates. This affects your running cost calculations.

**Government incentives** — check what your state offers before finalising your budget. Some states offer stamp duty exemptions that can save $1,500 to $3,000 on the purchase.

#### Step 3 — Explore the novated lease option before anything else

If you're an employee and your employer offers salary packaging — talk to a novated lease provider before you do anything else.

The federal FBT exemption means that a novated lease on a battery electric vehicle under $91,387 can save you anywhere from $5,000 to $15,000 compared to buying outright, depending on your income. The higher your tax bracket, the bigger the saving.

This single conversation — which takes 20 minutes — could be the most valuable thing you do in the entire buying process. Don't skip it.

#### Step 4 — Shortlist 2 to 3 models

Now you can start looking at cars. Based on your budget and lifestyle, narrow it down to 2 or 3 models to compare seriously.

Key specs to compare:

**Real-world range** — not WLTP. Search for Australian owner reports on YouTube and Reddit. Real-world range is typically 10 to 20% lower than the official figure.

**DC fast charging speed** — this matters for road trips. A car that charges at 50kW takes twice as long to top up as one that charges at 100kW. If you travel regionally, this is important.

**Warranty** — check both the vehicle warranty and the battery warranty separately. Battery warranties typically run 8 years but the capacity threshold at which they kick in varies.

**Service network** — how many service centres does the brand have in your city? For newer Chinese brands this is worth checking specifically.

#### Step 5 — Test drive properly

Book a test drive for each shortlisted model. But don't accept a 10-minute drive around the block.

Ask for an extended test drive that includes:

* Highway speeds — feel how the car handles and check real consumption at 100km/h
* Regenerative braking — test the different regen settings and see what feels natural
* Charging — if possible, plug in during the test so you understand the process

Drive each car on the same route so your comparison is fair. Pay attention to how the car actually feels, not just the specs on paper.

#### Step 6 — Negotiate properly

A few things to know before you sit down with a dealer:

**Get the drive-away price in writing** before any negotiation. This is your baseline.

**Ask about included extras** — home charger, tinted windows, floor mats. These are often easier to negotiate than price reductions.

**Check wait times** — if your preferred model has a 3-month wait, ask if there are demonstrator or stock models available sooner.

**Research resale values** — check Carsales for what similar models are selling for used. This gives you negotiating context and helps you understand what you're really paying.

**Never sign on the day of your first visit.** Take the paperwork home. Sleep on it. A good dealer will let you. A pushy dealer is a red flag.

#### Step 7 — Sort your home charging before delivery

This is the step most first-time EV buyers forget until it's too late.

Once you've signed, immediately:

1. Book a certified electrician to install your 7kW wallbox — allow 1 to 2 weeks for availability
2. Check if your dealer is including a home charger — if so, get the installation timeline confirmed
3. Switch your electricity plan to an EV-friendly tariff with cheap overnight rates

You want all of this sorted before your car arrives. Taking delivery of an EV and having no way to charge it properly at home is frustrating and entirely avoidable.

#### Step 8 — Download your apps before delivery day

Before you pick up your car, download:

* **Your brand's companion app** — most EVs now have an app for remote charging, pre-conditioning the cabin, and monitoring battery status
* **Chargefox** — one of Australia's largest charging networks
* **Evie Networks** — strong coverage in QLD and NSW
* **PlugShare** — maps every public charger in Australia with real-time status
* **ABRP (A Better Route Planner)** — essential for planning road trips

#### Step 9 — Delivery day checklist

When you take delivery, before you drive away:

* Confirm the charging cable is in the car
* Have the dealer walk you through the charging process hands-on
* Check that the home charging wallbox installation is confirmed if included
* Download the brand app and pair it to the car before leaving the dealership
* Confirm the warranty registration has been completed

#### Step 10 — The first week

The first week of EV ownership is when most habits get set. A few things to do immediately:

**Set your charging schedule** — most EVs let you set a timer so the car automatically charges during off-peak hours overnight. Set this up on day one.

**Charge to 80% for daily use** — only charge to 100% when you need maximum range. Daily charging to 80% is better for long-term battery health.

**Join an Australian EV community** — r/AusEV on Reddit and Facebook EV groups are excellent for real-world tips from Australian owners. You'll learn more in a week of reading than months of research elsewhere.

**Bookmark EVClear.com.au** — we publish ongoing guides, model reviews, incentive updates, and tools specifically for Australian EV buyers.

#### The honest summary

Buying an EV in 2026 is one of the best financial decisions most Australian drivers can make. The process has a few unique steps compared to buying a petrol car — but none of them are complicated once you know what to expect.

Follow these steps in order, take your time, and you'll drive away confident that you made the right call.
