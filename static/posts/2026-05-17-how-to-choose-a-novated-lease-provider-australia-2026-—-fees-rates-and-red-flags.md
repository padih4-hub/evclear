### Why It Matters More Than You Think

The novated lease structure itself is the same regardless of which provider you use — pre-tax salary deductions, FBT exemption on eligible EVs, running costs bundled in. That part doesn't change.

What changes is how much of your saving the provider takes in fees. A difference of $1,500-$2,000 in annual management fees over a 3-year lease is $4,500-$6,000 out of your pocket. That's not a rounding error — that's a significant chunk of the tax benefit you're supposed to be keeping.

- - -

### The Main Things to Compare

#### 1. Management Fees

Every novated lease provider charges a management fee — it's how they make money. The question is how much and how it's structured.

Some charge a flat annual fee ($400-$800 per year is typical). Some charge a percentage of the vehicle value. Some bury fees inside the interest rate or the bundled services pricing.

Always ask for a total annual management fee in dollar terms, not a percentage. Then compare that number across at least 2-3 providers before deciding.

#### 2. Interest Rate on the Finance Component

The lease has a finance component — essentially the cost of borrowing to fund the car. Providers set their own rates and they vary.

A difference of 1% on a $55,000 car over 3 years is roughly $825. Not huge but worth knowing. Ask each provider for the comparison rate, not just the advertised rate.

#### 3. What's Actually Included in the Package

Most providers offer a fully maintained novated lease — meaning rego, insurance, servicing, tyres, and charging costs are all bundled into your pre-tax deduction. But what's included varies.

Ask specifically:

* Is comprehensive insurance included or just CTP?
* What insurer do they use and can you see the PDS?
* Are tyres included or only covered after a certain threshold?
* How are servicing claims processed — do you pay upfront and claim back, or is it handled directly?

The claims process matters more than most people realise. Some providers make it easy — an app, a card, direct billing. Others make you pay out of pocket and wait for reimbursement. In a budget sense those are very different experiences.

#### 4. Residual Value

At the end of the lease you'll owe a residual — a lump sum set by the ATO as a percentage of the original vehicle value. For a 3-year lease it's typically around 46.88% of the purchase price.

On a $55,000 BYD Seal that's roughly $25,784. You can pay it out, refinance it into a new lease, or hand the car back.

What varies between providers is how they handle the residual at end of term. Some are flexible and will let you roll into a new lease easily. Others have exit fees or lock you into specific terms. Ask what happens at end of lease before you sign.

#### 5. Customer Service and Technology

This sounds soft but it matters. You'll be dealing with this provider for 3-5 years. Can you submit claims through an app? Do they have a dedicated account manager? How quickly do they respond when something goes wrong?

Check Google reviews and ProductReview.com.au for each provider before you commit. Look specifically for reviews about claims handling and customer service, not just the sales process.

### The One Thing Most People Skip

Get at least 2-3 quotes before you decide. This sounds obvious but most people don't do it — they go with whoever their employer suggests or whoever calls them first.

The quotes are free and there's no obligation. A 30-minute comparison exercise can save you thousands over the life of the lease.

When comparing quotes, make sure you're comparing like for like — same car, same term, same included services. Providers sometimes structure quotes differently to make their numbers look better. Ask each one for a total annual cost breakdown including all fees.

- - -

### Red Flags to Watch For

**Pressure to decide quickly** — a good provider won't push you to sign before you've had time to compare. Anyone creating urgency is trying to prevent you from shopping around.

**Vague answers on fees** — if a provider can't give you a clear dollar figure for annual management fees, that's a problem. Fees should be transparent and easy to understand.

**Insurance you can't verify** — ask for the PDS of any insurance included in the package. If they can't provide it, you don't know what you're actually covered for.

**Residual manipulation** — some providers set artificially low residuals to make the weekly payment look cheaper. That means a larger balloon payment at the end. Always check the residual percentage against ATO guidance.

### Frequently Asked Questions

**How many novated lease providers should I get quotes from?**

At least 2-3. The quotes are free and the difference in total cost can be significant. Always compare like for like on the same car and lease term.

**Can I choose my own novated lease provider?**

Sometimes — it depends on your employer. Some employers have a preferred provider arrangement. Ask HR whether you're free to use any provider or whether you need to use their recommended one.

**What's the most important thing to compare between providers?**

Total annual management fee in dollar terms. Everything else being equal, the provider with the lowest transparent fee structure will give you the best outcome.

**Is it worth using a specialist EV novated lease provider?**

Often yes — providers who specialise in EVs tend to have better knowledge of FBT eligibility, LCT thresholds, and the specific nuances of EV leasing. NLC is the most prominent specialist in Australia.

**Can I switch providers mid-lease?**

Generally no — once you've signed a novated lease it's with that provider for the term. This is why comparing upfront matters so much.
